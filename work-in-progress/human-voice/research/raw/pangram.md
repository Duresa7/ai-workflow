# Pangram Labs AI-text detector: mechanics, measured accuracy, failures, signals (notes through 2026-10-13)

Confidence key: **[high]** = primary source read directly; **[med]** = secondary source or abstract/summary only; **[low]** = unverified. **[V]** = vendor-reported figure; **[I]** = independent measurement.

## Summary

- Pangram (Pangram Labs, Brooklyn; founded 2023 by Max Spero and Bradley Emi, launched as Checkfor.ai Oct 2023, renamed Apr 2024) is a supervised transformer classifier, not a perplexity/burstiness tool. It is trained on ~28M pre-2022 human documents paired with "synthetic mirrors" (LLM text matched on topic, length, and style), then iteratively retrained on "hard negatives" (human text the current model misclassifies). [high, S1, S2]
- Model lineage: v1 (Feb 2024, "slightly modified transformer"); Pangram 3.0 (Dec 11 2025, EditLens-based, adds lightly/moderately AI-assisted labels); 3.1 (75-word minimum); 3.2 (Feb 27 2026, 50-word minimum, 22 languages); 3.3/3.3.1/3.3.2 (May 13-18 2026); Pangram 4 (Jul 29 2026, mixture-of-experts backbone ~6x larger, token-level labels, CRF segmentation, humanizer head, 24 languages). [high, S5, S6, S8, S9]
- Vendor headline numbers (Pangram 4): FPR 0.0041% (41 of 1,000,000 FineWeb English docs; ~1 in 24,000), FNR 0.3396% on 519,993 texts from 26 models, 98.83% of humanized text flagged as AI or Mixed. Pangram's marketing more commonly says "1 in 10,000" FPR and "99.98% accuracy". All are measured on Pangram's own held-out sets. [high, S9, S10, S11]
- Best independent evidence: Jabarian & Imas (Chicago Booth, Aug 2025) found Pangram FPR 0-0.0075 by genre and FNR 0.0045-0.038 across GPT-4.1/Claude Opus 4/Sonnet 4/Gemini 2.0 Flash, robust to StealthGPT, the only detector meeting an FPR<=0.005 cap. Russell et al. (ACL 2025) found Pangram matched a 5-expert majority vote (98-99.3% TPR, 2-2.7% FPR). VUB (IJEI, Jun 2026) found 0 false positives on 40 ESL theses and 95-97.5% recall on AI/hybrid/humanised theses where Turnitin/GPTZero/Copyleaks scored 0% on fully-AI. Epoch AI (Jul 2026) found 0/495 false positives. [high, S14, S16, S18, S19]
- GPTZero disputes the Booth ranking on recall only (it says the authors used the wrong API field); its own re-run has both tools at 0.05% FPR with GPTZero recall 99.3% vs Pangram 98.9%. [high, S15; vendor]
- Known weaknesses, confirmed by both vendor and independent sources: short text (<50 words refused; 50-150 words least reliable), text that mixes human and AI within a window (segments of 150-350 words were labeled wholesale as AI in 3.x), lightly AI-polished human text (Saha & Feizi: 42.6% of extremely-minor Llama2-7B polishes flagged), style-imitation prompts (Epoch: 10.1% missed), adversarial rhyme/repetition/archaic prose (Mantzarlis: 86% of 588 adversarial AI samples passed as human, pre-Pangram 4), poetry/recipes/lists, and context-dependence (same passage gets different verdicts alone vs embedded). [high, S9, S19, S20, S21, S22, S25]
- Documented high-profile false positives in 2026: Taylor Lorenz (acknowledged by Spero, blamed on a 50-word excerpt), three WSJ opinion pieces (Taranto: "defamation machine"), Freddie deBoer's 300-word passage (100% AI inside a 5,000-word essay rated 100% human), Charlie Finch's 226-word passage flipped by deleting one word, Katharine English's short story. Documented false negatives: Wagner's disclosed Claude-assisted essays rated 100% human; a Claude-drafted, humanizer-edited post rated 100% human. [high/med, S25-S31]
- Institutional use grew sharply in 2026: NeurIPS 2026 Position Paper Track desk-rejected 178 papers (18.4%) using Pangram 3.3.2 with the chairs' own window-size choice (default windows would have flagged 42.7% at >=90%, 100-word windows 12.7%); Substack added reader-facing scans (Jul 21 2026); NewsGuard built an AI-content-farm datastream on it (Mar 2026); AACR screens abstracts; ICLR 2026 review analysis (21% fully AI) came from Pangram's own EditLens. [high, S23, S33, S34, S35]
- Pangram is absent from the RAID public leaderboard (54 submissions, none Pangram as of Oct 2026); it self-reports a RAID random-sample result (99.26% accuracy, 0.01% FPR, 0.93% FNR, N=66,855, Pangram 3.3). [high, S12, S8]
- The company itself states: "All AI detection tools have a nonzero false positive rate," "we strongly discourage the use of our classifier as a sole arbiter of academic integrity," confidence labels are "not a calibrated probability estimate," and the model "cannot infer where an idea came from." [high, S1, S9, S5]

## How it works / Mechanics

### What the product outputs
- API (Pangram 4, `version: "4.0"`): `prediction_short` is `AI`, `Human`, or `Mixed`; `fraction_ai`, `fraction_ai_assisted`, `fraction_human` sum to 1; `windows[]` each carry `label` (`AI-Generated`, `AI-Assisted`, `Human Written`), `ai_assistance_score` (0 = fully human, 1 = fully AI), `confidence` (`High`/`Medium`/`Low`), `is_humanized`, `humanizer_score` (0-1, default threshold 0.91). Pangram 3.x used `ai_assistance_score` buckets: <=0.25 Human, 0.25-0.5 Lightly AI-assisted, 0.5-0.75 Moderately AI-assisted, >=0.75 AI. [high, S7, S8, S9]
- "AI fraction" as used in Pangram's public analyses = number of segments classified AI / total segments (not a probability). [high, S32]
- Pangram 4 model card: confidence "measures the peakedness of the unconstrained CRF posterior" and is "not a calibrated probability estimate"; `ai_assistance_score` "is likewise not the probability of the displayed label." [high, S9]
- Free users of 3.0 saw lightly-assisted text as "Human" and moderately-assisted as "AI-generated" by default; four-tier breakdown was a paid feature. [high, S5]
- Minimum input: 50 words (3.2 onward; 3.1 required 75). Rationale: "short texts don't carry enough signal for a reliable AI-or-human prediction"; example given is that "delve" "was used nearly 15 times more often in AI writing than human writing" but context is needed. [high, S6, S13]

### Architecture (by version)
- v1 (Feb 2024): "a slightly modified transformer-style architecture"; tokens -> embeddings -> classifier head outputting 0 (human) / 1 (AI). No parameter count disclosed. [high, S1, S4]
- DAMAGE humanizer detector (Jan 2025): Mistral NeMo (~12B) + LoRA, 512-token context; humanized data only 0.68% of the training set, oversampled 18x; only top-fluency ("L1") humanizers used because including worse ones hurt precision. [high, S3]
- Pangram 3 (Dec 2025): built on the EditLens architecture (regression model predicting degree of AI editing, trained with embedding-cosine and "soft n-grams" similarity as intermediate supervision; dataset 60,000 train with 303 editing prompts; human sources pre-2022 Amazon/Google reviews, Reddit WritingPrompts, FineWeb-EDU, XSum, CNN/DailyMail). Longer documents "break... into segments and classify each one within the context of the longer document." Pangram 3.3 segments are ~150-350 words (Spero, Jul 20 2026). [high, S5, S24, S26]
- Pangram 4 (Jul 29 2026): "a popular open-weight MoE model" (unnamed) with "6x as many parameters as Pangram 3.3"; shared backbone with four heads: a 15-way bucketed segment head (512-token segments), a 3-class tokenwise head (human / ai-assisted / ai-generated), a binary mixed-authorship head, and a 4-way humanizer head trained as a stop-gradient probe. "Repeat2": input is duplicated so every token attends to full context. Inference: overlapping 512-token windows with stride 256, a linear-chain CRF with Viterbi decoding, sentence majority voting, minimum-segment-length merging; scores "averaged and smoothed to discourage spurious flickering." Two-stage LoRA training. [high, S9, S10]
- "AI-assisted" in Pangram 4 = "an iteratively edited segment where no token can be definitively attributed to a single AI or human author." Resolution is coarse: minimum segment ~two sentences. [high, S9, S10]

### Training method
- Human corpus (v1): ~28M documents, "open source, freely available, licensed for commercial use, and dated 2021 or earlier": reviews 15M, books 7M, scientific papers 3M, Wikipedia 1M, Q&A 1M, news 500k, creative writing 300k, ESL 165k, student writing 23k, email 16k. ~4M held out for evaluation. Pangram 4: "All training data is licensed for commercial use"; "not trained on user-submitted text or customer API data"; no unauthorized crawls. [high, S1, S9]
- Synthetic mirrors: for each human document, an LLM generates a document that "matches the topic and length of the original." Three prompt types: single prompt (e.g., "a [star] review of the same business, ~N words"), double prompt (LLM writes a title from the essay, then an essay from the title), and completion prompts (continue an excerpt in matching style). An appended instruction suppresses titles/word counts; postprocessing filters discard outputs with obvious tells; examples under 50 words rejected. Pangram 4 generated mirrors "with 75 models." The stated goal is that the model learns LLM writing traits rather than topic or style differences. [high, S1, S4, S10]
- Hard negative mining / active learning: start with n = 40,000 examples per domain (360,000 total); train; score the full ~28M pool; sample up to 10,000 false positives per domain (~80,000 per round) plus an equal number of fresh mirrors; retrain; repeat "while M_i is better than M_{i-1}." Number of rounds not reported. Effect on held-out FPR (domain-weighted total): 2.29% -> 0.02%; Wikipedia 5.34% -> 0.05%; Email 6.60% -> 0.00%; News 0.55% -> 0.001%; Q&A 2.52% -> 0.009%. Blog framing: "we didn't just need more examples, we needed harder ones" and "reduce our false positive rates by a factor of 100." Pangram 4 adds an offline round on held-out human text, then "is retrained from scratch on the resulting combined dataset." [high, S1, S2, S10]
- Humanized/paraphrased text (DAMAGE): both human and AI documents are run through humanizers and chunked to ~300 words; humanized human text is labeled human so the model learns invariance to humanization rather than treating humanizer artifacts as an AI signal. Adversarial test: a GPT-4o humanizer fine-tuned on the detector's own false negatives reduced TPR from 100% to 93.2%. [high, S3]
- AI-assisted text (EditLens / Pangram 3+): created by "prompting AI models to make edits of various degrees to text that was originally human-written"; token labels assigned by lexical and semantic alignment; Pangram 4 uses "Soft N-Grams Labeling" for mixed text. Pangram 4 evaluation prompts vary edit extent from ~1/10 to ~3/4 of the text across Grammarly, Apple Intelligence, and several LLMs. [high, S5, S9, S24]
- False-positive measurement (Emi, Sep 15 2026): three tiers of held-out human sets, all pre-ChatGPT (2022): large holdouts of "approximately 10,000 to 10,000,000 examples per set," "VIP sets" of ~1,000 hand-verified examples, and "challenge sets" of 10-100 examples including previously reported false positives and poetry/recipes. Per-domain rates "from 0.0% for movie scripts and congressional transcripts to 0.23% for recipes"; academic essays 0.004%. The overall "1 in 10,000" and the Pangram 4 "1 in 24,000" are both used without reconciliation. [high, S11]

### Browser extension
- "Pangram AI Detection" Chrome extension (30,000 users, 4.5/5 from 26 ratings, v2.17.0 updated Sep 15 2026): inline badges reading "Human," "AI-Assisted," or "AI" on posts over 50 words on X, LinkedIn, Substack, Reddit, Medium; expanding a badge opens "feed health" in a sidebar with "a % breakdown of human vs. AI content across your feed"; right-click "Check for AI content" on highlighted text; "Scan for AI" button in Google Docs. Free tier covers only the text scanner; Feed Scanner and Docs are paid ($20/month web subscription). Data handled: "Website content." [high, S36, S37, S38]
- Observed display behavior (Watkins, May 2026): color-coded red (AI) / yellow (mixed, AI-assisted) / green (human); clicking a label opens a report "with limited detail about why." [high, S29]

### Stated limitations (vendor)
- Intended for "natural-language prose written in complete sentences," >=50 words. Out of scope / higher error: short conversational replies, single-fact answers, source code, tables of contents, reference sections, templated/automated writing, instructions and manuals, math-heavy text, bullet lists, outlines. Remove headers/footers; prefer raw text or .docx over PDF. [high, S9, S8, S11]
- "Pangram 3.2 is accurate to a resolution of approximately 50 words"; "shorter segments of human text interspersed with AI text may be classified as AI assisted" and Pangram "will not be able to distinguish the human and AI parts at the word- or sentence-level." [high, S6]
- Context dependence: "a subset of a longer text will receive different predictions depending on whether it is isolated or embedded in the surrounding document." Pangram 4 report: predictions are "to some degree a black box"; the model "does not account for people who intentionally write like LLMs or have absorbed LLM style," called "a major focus of our ongoing research." [high, S9, S10]
- Non-English: 24 officially supported languages in Pangram 4; FNR is notably higher in Urdu (5.32%), Persian (3.17%), Vietnamese (1.97%), French (1.78%); FPR highest in Ukrainian (0.036%). Pangram 3.3's Google-translated academic set had 0.17% FPR vs 0.02% native. [high, S9, S8]
- Errors "may result in such harms" as reputational damage; results "should be contextualized and not used alone to judge authorship." [high, S9]

## Measured accuracy and independent evaluations

### Vendor-reported benchmarks [V]
- v1 (Feb 2024): 1,976-document benchmark, 10 domains, 8 LLMs (GPT-3.5 Turbo 0301/1106, GPT-4 0613, GPT-4 Turbo 1106, Gemini Pro, Mistral 7B, Mixtral 8x7B, LLaMA 2 70B): 99.85% accuracy, 0.19% FPR (blog); paper claims "over 38 times lower error rates" than GPTZero/Originality/DetectGPT, GPTZero FNR 10.02%, Originality FPR 9.24%. Unseen open models recall at 1% FPR 99.61-100%; GPT-4o 100%, Claude 3 99.76%, LLaMA 3 99.97%. Held-out Enron email: FPR 0.8%, FNR ~2%, 98% accuracy vs GPTZero 89%, Originality 91%. ESL: TOEFL (91 essays, Liang et al. set) 0% FPR; ELLIPSE (3,907) 0; ICNALE (5,600) 0.09%; vs GPTZero updated model 7.7% (1.1% counting "Possible AI" as negative). [high, S1, S2]
- Pangram 3.0 (Dec 2025): "99.98% accuracy"; on human essays: 99.84% fully human, 0.14% lightly assisted ("1 in 700"), 0.013% moderately ("1 in 7,500"), 0.0064% fully AI ("1 in 15,000"). [high, S5]
- Pangram 3.2 / 3.3 model cards: Academic Writing (English) FPR 0.02% (N=62,971); Poetry 0.54% -> 0.49%; News multilingual 0.35% -> 0.03%; Chatbot Arena FNR 1.98% -> 1.50%; RAID random sample (N=66,855) 99.27%/99.26% accuracy, 0.07%/0.01% FPR, 0.90%/0.93% FNR. [high, S6, S8]
- Pangram 4 (Jul 2026): AUROC 0.9916; FPR 0.0041% (95% CI 0.0032-0.0050%) vs 3.3.2's 0.0539%; FNR 0.3396% (1,766/519,993; Wilson CI 0.3242-0.3558%) vs 1.9942%; by family: Claude 0.195%, GPT 0.316%, DeepSeek 0.388%, Gemini 0.498%; lowest single model Claude Sonnet 5 0.140%, highest Grok 4.3 0.605%; multilingual FPR 0.0014% (14/996,273 across 104 FineWeb2 languages), FNR 1.24% (190,149 gens, 18 languages); non-native English 1 FP in 24,586; AI-polished human text flagged AI 0.18% -> 0.01% ("18x reduction"); recall of Mixed on prompt-based edits 5.54% -> 55.01%; WildChat edits 12.62% -> 65.17%; tokenwise accuracy on interleaved text 64.18% -> 92.02%; humanized text: 97.67% labeled AI, 98.83% AI-or-Mixed across 13 commercial humanizers; humanizer head FPR 1.93%, FNR 4.43%; by English domain: Academic 0.019%, Poetry 0.078%, Recipes 0.049%, How-To 0.016%, Amazon reviews 0.000%. [high, S9, S10]
- Pangram's own re-runs of third-party sets (Pangram 4 report): UChicago set TPR@1%FPR full length 100.00% (Pangram 3: 99.86%, GPTZero 98.64%, Originality 94.15%); under 50 words 99.70% vs GPTZero 0.00%; humanized full-length 98.93% vs GPTZero 44.32%, Originality 28.49%; human controls at 50% threshold: 0/7,968 vs GPTZero 57/7,968 (0.72%), Originality 8/7,308 (0.11%). Epoch style-imitation re-run: FNR 2.86% (17/594). Places where Pangram 4 trails Pangram 3.3.2: DetectRL AUROC 97.02 vs 99.75; Sem-Detect peer-review TPR@0.1%FPR 95.5% vs 100%; OpAI-Bench light polish mean prediction 0.0001 vs actual AI fraction 0.2075 (i.e., it under-calls light polish). [high, S10]
- ICLR 2026 analysis (Emi, Nov 18 2025): 19,000+ papers, 70,000+ reviews; 21% (15,899) of reviews "fully AI-generated," >50% "some form of AI involvement"; 9% of papers >50% AI. Negative controls: 0% FPR on pre-2022 ICLR/NeurIPS papers (some may be in training data); EditLens on 2022 reviews: ~1/1,000 confusions light-vs-human, 1/5,000 medium, 1/10,000 heavy, none fully-AI-vs-human; "1 in 100,000 on held-out arXiv papers." Caveat: EditLens was in private beta and unverifiable. Sem-Detect (May 2026) applied a different method to the same reviews and got 5% AI / 61% refined / 34% human vs EditLens 24/32/44, with no ground truth to adjudicate. [high, S35; med, S41]

### Independent evaluations [I]
- **Jabarian & Imas, "Artificial Writing and Automated Detection," Chicago Booth BFI WP 2025-116 (Aug 26 2025; NBER w34223).** 1,992 pre-2020 human passages (200 Amazon reviews, 200 blogs, 300 news, 1,000 novel excerpts, 100 restaurant reviews, 192 resumes) mirrored by GPT-4.1, Claude Opus 4, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.0 Flash. Pangram API v2025-05 (`prob_ai`), GPTZero API v2 (`average_generated_prob`), Originality Turbo, RoBERTa-base. FPR by genre (Pangram / GPTZero / Originality / RoBERTa): Amazon 0.0050/0.0238/0.0170/0.3063; blog 0/0.0050/0/0.6488; news 0.0008/0.0100/0.0026/0.7775; novel 0/0.0045/0.0025/0.5315; restaurant 0.0075/0.0100/0.0218/0.5175; resume 0/0/0.0013/0.6914. FPR across raw thresholds 0.1-0.9: Pangram 0.0010 -> 0.0000; GPTZero flat 0.0071; Originality 0.0027 -> 0.0011. FNR (Pangram) 0.0045-0.0382 by model/threshold; GPTZero 0.002-0.030; Originality up to 0.424. Stubs <50 words (160 per model): Pangram FPR mostly 0, max 0.025; AUROC 0.963-1.0; Originality refused many stubs. StealthGPT rewrites: Pangram "detects nearly 100%" on long passages; GPTZero FNR ~0.50+; Originality up to 0.21. Cost per correctly flagged AI passage: Pangram $0.0228, Originality $0.0416, GPTZero $0.0575. "Pangram is the only detector that meets a stringent policy cap (FPR <= 0.005) without compromising the ability to accurately detect AI text." Funding: Booth CAAI, BFI, Google Cloud Research Program; "no financial or personal conflicts of interest." Working paper, not peer-reviewed. [high, S14]
- **GPTZero rebuttal (Jan 12 2026) [vendor].** Says Booth used `average_generated_prob` ("should not be used for binary classification") instead of `class_probabilities`; re-ran with GPTZero model 2025-12-18-base, Pangram `prediction`, Originality `classification`: FPR GPTZero 0.05% (1 error), Pangram 0.05% (1 error), Originality 0.11%; recall GPTZero 99.3% (52 errors), Pangram 98.9% (85 errors), Originality 81.3% (only 6,879 of 9,960 predictions processed). Binary mappings were chosen by GPTZero. [high, S15]
- **Russell, Karpinska, Iyyer, ACL 2025 (arXiv 2501.15654, Jan 26 2025).** 300 nonfiction English articles; generators GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, GPT-4o paraphrased, o1-pro, o1-pro humanized. Overall TPR (FPR): expert majority vote 99.3 (0); Pangram Humanizers 99.3 (2.7); Pangram 98.0 (2); GPTZero 85.3 (0.7); Fast-DetectGPT 80.0 (7.2); Binoculars (accuracy mode) 66.7 (1.3); RADAR 15.3 (2). On o1-pro humanized: Pangram 90.0 (6.7); Pangram Humanizers 96.7 (10); GPTZero 46.7 (3.3); Binoculars 6.67 (0). Note the 2.0-2.7% FPR here is far above Pangram's self-reported rates (N=150 human articles). Acknowledgments thank "Pangram and GPTZero for providing" API access; first author Jenna Russell later joined Pangram (listed as Pangram Research Scientist in May 2026; co-author of the Pangram 4 report). [high, S16]
- **Van Vlasselaer, van Droogenbroeck & Spruyt, IJEI (Jun 29 2026), preregistered AsPredicted #228383.** 160 English social-science theses of 4,000-12,000 words: 40 human (pre-2019, non-native English graduate students), 40 fully AI (ChatGPT Deep Research, May 2025), 40 hybrid (literature review + conclusion replaced by GPT-4o; median 44.6% AI), 40 humanised (hybrid rewritten by GPT-4o "to sound more like a student and raise perplexity and burstiness"). Recall fully-AI (inclusive/strict): Pangram 97.5%/65%; Turnitin 0% (all scored 0-20%); GPTZero 0%; Copyleaks 0%. Hybrid: Pangram 95% (37/40), Turnitin 60%, Copyleaks 30%, GPTZero 0%. Humanised: Pangram 95%, Turnitin 50%, Copyleaks 22.5%, GPTZero 2.5%. False positives on ESL human theses: 0 for Pangram, Turnitin, Copyleaks; GPTZero "small." Mixed-effects error (ground truth minus score) on fully-AI: Pangram -14.32, GPTZero -85.70, Copyleaks -85.97, Turnitin -99.63. Then 1,163 real 2024-25 master's theses scanned with Pangram only: 529 (45.5%) flagged, mean AI estimate 34.0%, median 30%, >80% rare; no ground truth. Conclusion: "only [Pangram] produced satisfactory results," but treat as "an initial flag" not sole evidence. Tool versions not reported. [high, S18]
- **Epoch AI, Jaeho Lee (Jul 15 2026).** 495 human passages (~500 words, 99 authors; blogging/fiction/scientific) and 297 AI passages per condition from Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro. Pangram 3.3.2 (default verdict; "Mixed" counted as non-detection): FP 0/495; FN basic prompt 0/297; FN style imitation (model shown 5 of the author's passages) 30/297 = 10.10% (scientific 25/99, blogging 4/99, fiction 1/99; Gemini 20/99, Claude 5/99, GPT-5.5 5/99); counting Mixed as detection: 6.1%. GPTZero: FP 0/495, FN basic 2/297, style imitation 32/297 (10.77%). [high, S19]
- **Reuters Institute, Marina Adami (Jul 17 2026).** Alexios Mantzarlis (Indicator) built 588 AI samples exploiting preliminary weaknesses (rhyme, repetition, archaic language); Pangram labeled them human "86% of the time." - **Karr, Khvatskii, Hua, Chawla (Notre Dame), arXiv 2608.11256 (Aug 6 2026).** Published English abstracts 2013-15 vs 2023-25, commercial detector (Wikipedia identifies it as Pangram 3.2), threshold 0.50: light "refine abstract only" edits flagged 38-80%; unmodified 2023-25 originals flagged 9-15% (non-STEM far above STEM); after Undetectable AI humanization detection <4% (FNR >96%). Scores track "long-token and Academic Word List density, not authorship intent alone"; "detector scores should not serve as standalone misconduct evidence." [high, S22]
- **NeurIPS 2026 Position Paper Track chairs (Jun 2 2026).** Pangram v3.3.2 on 969 submissions: 273 (28.2%) scored 100%; 178 (18.4%) desk-rejected; 123 (12.7%) required to show "substantial human engagement." Windows flagged if AI probability > 0.75; with default 250-350-word windows 42.7% of papers scored >=90%; with ~100-word windows 12.7%; chairs chose medium windows "to reduce over-claiming." They state a 100% score "should not be interpreted as '100% of the text is AI-generated'" and cite audits "reported a false positive rate of less than 0.1%" (no details). Appeal requires version history with pre-AI and post-AI checkpoints. [high, S23]
- **Saha, Juneja, Chaudhuri, Sajeevan, Shah, Pruthi, arXiv 2603.20450 (ICML 2026).** Five detectors including two commercial (not named in abstract) "misclassify a non-trivial fraction of LLM-polished reviews as AI-generated"; "none meets the accuracy standards required for identifying AI use in peer reviews." Pangram 4 report's own re-run on this set: 1.4%/2.5% FPR on human-AI-polished reviews (3.3.2: 14.9%). [med/high, S40, S10]
- **Allaham & Diakopoulos, AIES 2026 (arXiv 2605.23684).** Search-engine citation audit; validation on 200 human + 200 AI RAID texts reportedly yielded 100% accuracy for both Pangram and GPTZero (small sample; from search summary, abstract does not state it). [low/med, S42]
- **RAID public leaderboard (raid-bench.xyz / liamdugan/raid):** 54 submissions as of Oct 2026 (gptzero, grammarly, winston-ai, binoculars, etc.); no Pangram entry. Pangram's DAMAGE paper reports RAID TPR at 5% FPR of 93.0% (paraphrase) and 97.0% (synonym) and model cards report a self-run RAID random sample (above). [high, S12, S3, S8]
- **Journalism tests:** NYT Brian X. Chen (Aug 2026) found the text detector separated human from AI in his trials including style-imitation; image detector weaker [med, S43]. TechCrunch's Rebecca Bellan (Jul 29 2026): "very impressive but not perfect"; it flagged sentences she had fully rewritten and gave 13% AI-assisted on a polished article [high, S37]. Tom's Guide and ZDNET: qualitative praise, no figures [med, S17].

## Documented failures and false positives

- **Taylor Lorenz (Vanity Fair journalist), spring 2026.** Accused on social media based on a circulating Pangram score; Spero investigated and acknowledged a false positive, attributing it to a 50-word excerpt. Lorenz: "Thank god for edit history." (The Atlantic, Matteo Wong, May 30 2026, "America Has a Pangram Problem"; via Wikipedia/mirror). [med, S25, S44]
- **Wall Street Journal, April 2026.** Pangram flagged three WSJ opinion columns; editorial features editor James Taranto reran them, got different scores, contacted the authors, and called Pangram a "defamation machine." ## Linguistic signals (what research says separates human from LLM prose, as it bears on Pangram)

- Pangram does not publish its features; it says the model picks up "subtle cues in the writing" and "stylistic word-choice signals," needs word-in-context (its example: "delve" ~15x more frequent in AI writing), and "cannot infer where an idea came from." [high, S4, S5, S13]
- Russell et al. expert-clue taxonomy (share of explanations mentioning each): vocabulary 53.1% ("LLMs use specific words and phrases more often... repetitive, unnatural, or overly complex wording"); sentence structure 35.9% ("not only... but also," "it's not just this, it's this," consistently listing three items, uniform length); grammar & punctuation 24.8% (AI "grammatically perfect," avoiding dashes and ellipses; humans show "dashes, brackets, quotes intermixed... short spurts"); originality 23.7% (AI "straightforward, 'safe,' and lacking in surprises or humor"); quotes 22.3% (AI quotes "overly formal... mirror the article's main text too closely"); clarity 19.5% (AI over-explains, "telling" not "showing"). The annotators' guide lists AI vocabulary (delve, tapestry, testament, multifaceted, crucial, pivotal, vibrant, seamlessly, underscores, navigate, foster, realm, landscape, "in a world where," "it's important to note," "step-by-step"), formatting tells (bold-header bullets with colons, always-italicized titles, repetitive headers), tone (flowery, formal, "inherently positive," uplifting conclusions), scene-setting intros ("On a drab November morning..."), neat summarizing conclusions, avoidance of controversy and swearing, and weaker factual specificity. Vocabulary clues drop from 57.1% to 42.3% when generators are told to avoid AI vocab, but structure/originality clues persist. [high, S16]
- Human features that correlate with Pangram false positives: deBoer's Pangram 4 false positive used a hedged, position-free corporate topic; five-paragraph structure; heavy transitional words; ChatGPT-style vocabulary; triads; uniform sentence length and rhythm; "not just X, it's Y" contrasts with em-dashes; no anecdotes, names, numbers or personal detail; perfect grammar and no contractions; chatbot formatting (bold bullets, parallel headers); and stylistic consistency across the whole document (because scores aggregate over sliding windows). Karr et al.: scores rise with long-token and Academic Word List density. Slate's Alex Kirshner reports Pangram flagging single-digit percentages of his drafts and his own em-dash use falling ~50% as he edits to "sound less like A.I." [high, S27, S22, S53]
- Features that cause Pangram misses: humanizer paraphrase (older versions), rhyme/repetition/archaic diction (Mantzarlis), style imitation from an author's samples especially in scientific prose (Epoch), pre-1930s-style generators (Talkie), heavy multi-round human editing of AI drafts (Zieminski 7% detection after 5-7 rounds), and polishing by newer models (Saha & Feizi: older/smaller polishers are more detectable). Pangram concedes it "does not account for people who intentionally write like LLMs or have absorbed LLM style." [high, S20, S19, S29, S31, S21, S10]
- Length: everyone agrees the signal degrades under ~100-150 words. Booth found Pangram FPR up to 0.025 on <50-word stubs; Pangram refuses <50 words; Spero attributed the Lorenz error to a 50-word excerpt. [high, S14, S13; med, S44]
- Score behavior: Pangram's document score is a segment fraction and tends to be bimodal ("almost always predicts a score very close to 0 or 1" per EditLens; deBoer saw only 100%/0% verdicts), so intermediate "percentages" should not be read as probability of AI authorship. [high, S24, S26]
