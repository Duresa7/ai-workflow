# GPTZero: mechanics, measured accuracy, documented failures (research notes through 2026-10-13)

Compiled 2026-10-13. Confidence tags: **high** = primary source read directly; **medium** = secondary source or primary source read only in part; **low** = unverified/aggregator. "Vendor" marks GPTZero's own claims; "independent" marks third-party measurement. Scope is mechanisms, measured accuracy, failures, and linguistic signals only; no evasion material.

## Summary

- GPTZero launched 2 Jan 2023 as a Princeton student side project by Edward Tian (co-founder Alex Cui); ~30,000 uses in the first week crashed it [S1, S2]. By mid-2024 it claimed 4M users; at its June 2026 acquisition by Superhuman (ex-Grammarly) Tian told Business Insider it had >19M registered users and ~$30M ARR on only $13.5M raised [S31, S32].
- The original 2023 model scored two statistics computed with a GPT-2-class language model: **perplexity** (how predictable the text is to the LM; low = AI-like) and **burstiness** (how much perplexity/sentence structure varies across the document; low = AI-like) [S3, S1].
- GPTZero itself says it stopped relying on those metrics in autumn 2023 and "migrated to a deep-learning based architecture"; perplexity/burstiness survive only as one of "seven indicators" [S3]. The June 2023 interim model already combined statistics with a deep-learning head (vendor-reported AUC 0.85 -> 0.95 on GPT-4 text) [S5].
- The current system (documented in a Feb 2026 arXiv paper by GPTZero staff + Penn State's Dongwon Lee) is a proprietary supervised deep classifier with a hierarchical taxonomy: top level Human / AI / Mixed; under AI: Pure AI / Polished / AI-Paraphrased; it emits per-sentence AI probabilities in one forward pass, plus a "Deep Scan" sentence-attribution layer (saliency/occlusion) [S6]. The web UI calls the highlighting "Advanced Scan"; sentences are coloured green -> yellow -> orange by confidence [S13].
- Outputs are a document-level probability, a three-way label (API: HUMAN_ONLY / MIXED / AI_ONLY), and a confidence bucket (uncertain / moderately / highly confident). GPTZero says the calibration step is deliberately biased toward false negatives over false positives [S4].
- Vendor accuracy claims have escalated: "99% accuracy, FPR no more than 1%" (2023-25); 96.5% on mixed docs (Aug 2024); "1 false positive in 10,402 student essays" and FPR <0.03% on its own frontier-model benchmark for "GPTZero 4o" (Sept 2026) [S4, S7, S8].
- Independent measurements are far more mixed and strongly dataset-dependent: Weber-Wulff et al. 2023 ranked it 8th of 14 (54% binary accuracy) and found half its positive calls on human/translated text would be false accusations [S14]; RAID (ACL 2024) scored it 66.5% at 5% FPR, with 24-44% on several base models but 97-99% on ChatGPT/GPT-4 [S15]; Chicago Booth/NBER 2025 found a flat 0.71% FPR, AUROC 0.993, but FNR >=0.50 after a "humanizer" [S16]; Epoch AI (July 2026) found 0/495 false positives but 10.8% misses on author-imitation prompts (24% in scientific prose) [S17].
- The best-documented false-positive classes: canonical texts (US Constitution, Bible passages) in 2023 [S18]; non-native-English writing (Liang et al. 2023: 61.22% average FPR on TOEFL essays across seven detectors incl. GPTZero) [S19]; machine-translated text [S14]; and student cases (UC Davis, 2023; Rignol v. Yale, 2025-26) [S20, S21].
- GPTZero's position on reliability: "no AI detection tool is perfect," results are "a conversation starter, and not as the final verdict," "should not be used to punish" [S4, S13, S22]. On ESL bias it claims a re-run of the Stanford code flags 1 of 91 TOEFL essays (1.1%) with a further 6.6% "possible AI" [S23]; Pangram's report reads the same numbers as 7.7% FPR unless "possible AI" is counted as negative [S24].
- 2024-26 feature additions: Authorship/Writing Report (Sept 2024), AI Vocabulary top-50 list (Oct 2024), hallucination detector, AI Vision social-feed labeller (Feb 2026), AI Patterns (Aug 2026), "GPTZero 4o" model (Sept 2026), Model 4.11b (7 Oct 2026) [S25-S30, S8, S9].
- Linguistic signals the literature associates with LLM prose: low perplexity/uniform sentence rhythm; 2-5x more present-participial clauses and ~2x more nominalizations than humans (instruction-tuned models); vocabulary used 10-200x more often ("tapestry," "delve," "showcasing," "plays a significant role in shaping"); higher lexical diversity + lower entropy for generated text, but lexical density for AI-edited text; rhetorical tics such as "not X but Y," triplets, inflated importance, phantom experts [S33, S34, S35, S26, S27].

## How it works / Mechanics

### 1. The 2023 statistical model: perplexity and burstiness
- Edward Tian's 1 Mar 2023 explainer: perplexity is "how likely an AI model would have chosen the exact same set of words as found in the document"; "a perplexity above 85 is more likely than not from a human source," with the caveat that there is no absolute scale. Burstiness is "a measure of how much writing patterns and text perplexities vary over the entire document"; LMs produce more uniform text, so low burstiness is AI-like. Together they "formed the first layer" of the original model; the perplexity component used "a language model similar to ChatGPT" [S3, high].
- NPR, 9 Jan 2023: perplexity = how complex/surprising the text is to the bot; burstiness = variation between sentences ("humans tend to mix long and short sentences"); Tian admitted it was "not foolproof" [S1, high]. Reporting at the time said the LM used was GPT-2 [S2, medium].
- Why this fails on canonical text: Ars Technica (July 2023) quoted Margaret Mitchell: "Perplexity is a function of 'how surprising is this language based on what I've seen?'" Any text that appears heavily in training data (the Constitution, scripture) is low-perplexity and gets flagged [S18, high via mirror].
- Pangram's 2024 technical report notes the perplexity approach "performs best on the open-source models," since exact perplexity can only be computed white-box, and "GPTZero and Originality completely fail when asked to detect AI content produced by GPT-4" (Feb 2024 snapshot) [S24, high].

### 2. Transition to deep learning (June-autumn 2023)
- Vendor post dated 2 Jul 2023 ("Deep Learning Model Updates"): model version "2023-06-30" "combined our previous architecture and statistical discriminators approaches with a traditional deep learning approach"; "0.95 AUC compared to a previous 0.85 AUC in a challenging GPT4 dataset"; "99% accuracy in classifying human data as human"; "updated bi-weekly" [S5, high].
- Update note on the perplexity page: "As of autumn 2023, GPTZero no longer uses perplexity and burstiness for its AI detection because we migrated to a deep-learning based architecture." The old statistics are now "one of seven 'indicators'" alongside "novel text search and deep learning detection approaches"; the other indicators are not enumerated [S3, high].
- Technology page (2026): "end-to-end deep learning approach" trained on web, education, and multi-LLM text; a five-stage pipeline (input handling -> deep learning -> sentence classifier -> "Paraphraser Shield" -> output); supports pasted text, .docx, PDF, images, up to 50 files [S4, high].

### 3. The current architecture (arXiv 2602.13042, 13 Feb 2026)
- Authors: G. A. Adam, A. Cui, E. Thomas, E. Napier, N. Shmatko, J. Schnell, J. J. Tian, A. Dronavalli, E. Tian (GPTZero) and Dongwon Lee (Penn State); also lists Waterloo, Vector Institute, Mila affiliations [S6, high].
- "Architecture and hyperparameters are proprietary." Supervised deep model with a hierarchical multi-task head: L0 = {Human, AI, Mixed}; L1 under AI = {Pure AI, Polished, AI Paraphrased}. "Polished" = a human document run through an LLM for grammar/clarity, kept only if the Levenshtein ratio to the source falls within set bounds. Loss L = L_d + alpha*L_s (document cross-entropy + sentence binary cross-entropy). A post-hoc remapping r: R^3 -> R^3 improves calibration and reduces false positives [S6, high].
- Training data (Table 4): News 16M docs; Web Articles 8M; Encyclopedia 2.8M; Academic 1.25M; Essay 234K; Reviews 173K; Q&A 65K; Conversation 32K; sources are public datasets plus a proprietary generation pipeline over OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and open-source LLMs. Internal eval set: 40k docs (40% human / 40% AI / 20% mixed). Non-English volume "nearly the same" as English; 24-language case study [S6, high].
- Sentence highlighting: per-sentence AI probabilities emitted in a single forward pass; "Deep Scan" adapts Saliency and Occlusion to assign each sentence a score delta_k for its effect on P(AI|doc). The technology page separately says the first sentence-highlighting model was built on Hidden Markov Models [S6, S4, high].
- Adversarial training/red-teaming: paraphrase+translation chains up to four languages; Dipper and TempParaphraser paraphrasers; nine black-box "humanizer" services; white-box gradient-guided word substitution under a perplexity constraint. On a 1,000-text "bypasser" set the paper reports recall 93.5% (GPTZero) vs 57.3% (Originality) vs 49.7% (Pangram) — vendor-run [S6, high].
- Stated limitations (Sec. 7): in-distribution metrics "overly optimistic"; blind spots for low-quality LLMs (GPT-2, OPT); Deep Scan results "correlational, not causal"; adversarial training trades off in-distribution accuracy; non-standardised datasets risk cherry-picking [S6, high]. ESL/non-native handling is not addressed in the paper.

### 4. Output semantics: probability, mixed class, confidence
- Since 29 Dec 2023 / the 17 Jan 2024 UI, the model "sorts text into three classifications" (entirely human / entirely AI / mixed); the old binary "AI" number included mixed text, so website scores "might look lower than on our chrome extension," which still used the older model at the time [S10, high].
- The headline percentage is the model's probability that the whole document is AI, "not the share of the document that is AI"; proportion is conveyed by sentence highlights. Each scan gets a certainty score: high certainty = "we have seen texts similar in tone, style ... and format"; low certainty = "very dissimilar from ones in our training set ... should be interpreted with caution" [S10, S11, high].
- Technology page: confidence buckets uncertain / moderately confident / highly confident (API: low/medium/high); "high-confidence categories have an average error rate below 1%" (internal eval); an output mapping "biases the detector to prefer making less-harmful false-negative errors over false-positive errors" [S4, high].
- Jan 2024 interpretable-scores post: a post-hoc calibration keeps scores from being overconfident; it "advises against punitive action based solely on one scan" and recommends building a history of scans [S11, high].
- API (v2) field names seen in the literature: `average_generated_prob` (used by Chicago Booth), `highlight_sentence_for_ai`, document_classification HUMAN_ONLY/MIXED/AI_ONLY [S16, S4, high]. GPTZero's Jan 2026 rebuttal says Chicago Booth "did not use the correct field in our API for the AI probability" [S36, high].

### 5. Model release cadence 2025-26
- Model 3.7b, 14 Aug 2025: "complete overhaul of our training data" (GPT-4.1/o3/Gemini 2.5/Claude Sonnet 4 outputs, deep-research text, grammar-app-edited human text, RL-discovered adversarial prompts); recall at 1% FPR 89.9% (o3) to 99.1% (Claude Sonnet 4); "generalizes to GPT5 without explicit training" (95.0% recall) [S9, high, vendor].
- Epoch AI tested model string "2026-05-11-base" in July 2026 [S17].
- "GPTZero 4o" (= Model 4.10b per release notes), default since 20 Sept 2026, announced 24 Sept 2026: scaled-up academic/social-media training data, architecture "revamped to be more robust to paraphrasing," new "Masked Detection" (headers excluded, grey highlight), "designed to be indifferent to punctuation changes" such as em dashes or semicolons [S8, high, vendor].
- Model 4.11b "aka 4.1o", 7 Oct 2026: "Improved recall on sub-classes AI paraphrased, mixed and polished. Improved performance on third party benchmarks. Improved document chunking and aggregation. Improved AI detection on Claude and Gemini models" [S37, medium — release-notes page did not render; text from search index].

### 6. ESL de-biasing (vendor description)
- 24 Oct 2023 post "ESL Bias in AI Detection is an Outdated Narrative" (Alex Adam, Alex Cui): three methods — a CNN "parameter tagging" layer with an education tag; a pre-classifier that predicts whether a text is likely from an ESL writer and passes that to the detector; and added ESL-representative data plus sentence-level training. Re-running the Stanford authors' code on 19 Oct 2023: 1 of 91 TOEFL essays "AI-generated" (1.1%), 6.6% "possible AI," 92.3% human; "<2% false positive rate" on a larger internal ESL set [S23, high, vendor].
- Technology page (2026) restates "ESL/TOEFL texts: false positive rate reduced to 1.1%" and claims GPTZero is "the only detector specifically de-biased for ESL writers" [S4, vendor].

### 7. AI Vocabulary (Oct 2024-) and AI Patterns (Aug 2026)
- AI Vocabulary, released 7 Oct 2024: a list of the 50 (later ~100) words/phrases most over-represented in AI text, derived from "3.3 million texts," matched to human documents by subject and length; the top 50 are used "from 10x to 200x+ more." The page says the tool is separate from the AI probability: "A text can be scored as human and still contain phrases AI commonly uses," and changing those words "may or may not affect the AI score" [S26, S27, high]. Launch-post multipliers: "play a significant role in shaping" 182x; "today's fast-paced world" 107x; "notable works include" >120x; "showcasing" 20x; "remarked" 18x; "aligns" 16x; "surpassing" 12x; "tragically" 11x [S27, high, vendor].
- AI Patterns, 19 Aug 2026 (Advanced Scan): ten rhetorical/stylistic patterns seeded from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" guide and ranked by AI-vs-human frequency multiplier in GPTZero's database: Overblown Importance ("marks a pivotal moment"), Name Dropping, Empty Commentary, Sales-pitch Tone ("nestled in the heart of"), Phantom Experts ("experts argue"), Dressed-up Verbs ("serves as"), Not just X but Y, Everything in Threes, Unnecessary Caveats, Chatbot Language ("As an AI language model"). GPTZero: none is exclusive to AI; "a pattern is not proof of AI authorship" [S25, high, vendor].

### 8. Origin / Chrome extension, AI Vision, and Authorship
- Origin launched spring 2023 (Substack, 16 May 2023, demoed to ex-Reuters/NYT/BBC executives) as a browser extension to check "whether the content you are reading on the internet was created by a human or a machine" [S28, S29, high]. The Web Store listing is now titled "GPTZero: AI Detection & Writing Replay" (same ID), ~500,000 users, 4.7/5 from 737 ratings, v2026.10.1 updated 2 Oct 2026 [S30, high].
- What a reader sees (gptzero.me/chrome): scans run on request from the GPTZero menu on any readable page; a percent readout (mockup "34% AI probability — This text is likely a mix of human writing and AI generated text"); a "Show AI sentences" toggle colouring sentences "green for human, yellow to orange as confidence climbs"; "Highlights are a visual layer only: nothing is inserted." In Gmail incoming mail is labelled AI / Mixed / Human; in Google Docs it adds auto-scans, live highlighting, grading, and the Writing Report. The page carries the caveat "Results should not be used to punish or as the final verdict" [S13, high].
- AI Vision (26 Feb 2026): auto-labels LinkedIn, X and Reddit posts in-feed as Human / AI Detected / AI Polished / Mixed; no accuracy claims given [S38, high].
- Authorship / Writing Report (announced 18 Sept 2024): word/sentence-level AI-likely / human / mixed breakdown; report of time spent editing, number of editors, copy-paste history, and "secure video playback of typing patterns"; a shareable report writers can use "to validate their authenticity"; free for teachers and students that school year [S39, high]. Aug 2025 post: replay shows text "written, edited, and pasted," per-editor contribution "down to the percentage," a "typing analysis" that runs detection on large pastes; SOC2/FERPA [S40, high].

## Measured accuracy and independent evaluations

### Vendor claims (GPTZero's own benchmarks; not independent)
- Technology page: 99% accuracy AI vs human; FPR "no more than 1%"; 96.5% accuracy on mixed documents; ESL FPR 1.1%; "performance is strongest on longer texts and English prose" [S4].
- Benchmarking-methodology post, 30 Jan 2025: internal sets of "hundreds to thousands" of texts; models retrained monthly; Penn State lab reviews; mixed docs (Aug 2024) 96.5% acc / 0.9% FPR / 4.4% FNR; ChatGPT-o1 set: GPTZero 98.6% acc / 0.0% FPR vs Pangram 93.6% / 5.2% FPR; the 2023 Turnitin comparison table lists GPTZero FPR 2%. "If someone is claiming 100% accuracy, there is likely a flaw with their data or evaluation methodology" [S41].
- vs Copyleaks/Originality (25 Sept 2025), 3,000 samples 50/50: GPTZero 99.3% acc / 0.24% FPR / 98.8% recall; Copyleaks 90.7% / 5.26%; Originality 83.0% / 4.79%. Dataset and prompts not released [S42]. vs Pangram (9 Oct 2025), same set: GPTZero 99.6% / 0.13% FPR / 99.4% recall vs Pangram 97.5% / 0.20% / 95.4% [S43].
- GPTZero 4o (24 Sept 2026): PERSUADE student essays 1 FP in 10,402; Epoch AI set FPR 0.0% / FNR 2.69% (Pangram 4: 0.0% / 2.86%); Graphite web-article set FPR 1.36% / FNR 0.07%; DetectRL F1 average 96.53 vs Pangram 95.30; internal frontier set FPR <0.03% with recall 99.89% (GPT-5.6, n=10,898), 99.87% (Gemini 3.6, n=8,452), 99.37% (Grok 4.5), 99.04% (Claude 5, n=11,707) [S8].
- Chicago Booth re-run (12 Jan 2026): GPTZero 0.05% FPR / 99.3% recall / 99.5% acc vs Pangram 99.1% and Originality 85.0% after re-scoring the public corpus with what GPTZero says is the correct API field [S36].
- arXiv 2602.13042 tables (GPTZero 4.1b vs Pangram 3.1 vs Originality lite-102, AUC/acc/recall at 1% FPR): abstracts 99.9/99.4/99.2 vs 92.8/93.6/87.2; creative writing 99.9/98.7/97.4 vs 96.1/98.0/96.0; essays 99.9/99.8/99.7 vs 99.9/99.8/99.8; paper reviews 99.9/99.9/99.7; product reviews 99.9/99.2/98.3. Polished texts: 4,175 of 4,631 correctly labelled polished (247 -> AI, 205 -> human) [S6].

### Independent evaluations (chronological)
- **Liang et al., Patterns, Apr-Jul 2023 (Stanford)** — seven detectors (Originality, Quil, Sapling, OpenAI, Crossplag, GPTZero, ZeroGPT) on 91 TOEFL essays + 88 US 8th-grade essays: "misclassified over half of the TOEFL essays as 'AI-generated' (average false positive rate: 61.22%)"; 18/91 (19.78%) flagged unanimously; 89/91 flagged by at least one; "near-perfect accuracy for US 8-th grade essays." Prompting ChatGPT to "Elevate the provided text by employing literary language" cut detection of its own college essays "from 100% to 13%." Per-detector TOEFL rates appear only in Fig. 1a (image), not in text [S19, high].
- **Ibrahim et al., arXiv 2305.13934, May 2023 (32 university courses)** — on real student submissions GPTZero's false positive rate was 18% and false negative rate 32%; conclusion: "current AI-text classifiers cannot reliably detect ChatGPT's use in school work" [S44, medium — figures from secondary summaries; abstract page confirms only the conclusion].
- **Weber-Wulff et al., Int. J. Educ. Integrity, 2023** — 14 tools, 54 documents (9 each: human, machine-translated, two AI sets, manually edited AI, paraphrased AI). GPT Zero binary accuracy 54% (29/54), rank 8; semi-binary 66%; it was the only tool that did *not* lean human: it flagged 3/9 human and 6/9 machine-translated docs, giving a 50% "false accusation ratio" (worst of 14) while missing only 8.3% of AI docs (best). Verbatim: "For GPT Zero, half of the positive classifications would be false accusations, which makes this tool unsuitable for the academic environment." Overall: tools are "neither accurate nor reliable" [S14, high].
- **Bloomberg Businessweek, 18 Oct 2024** — GPTZero and Copyleaks on 500 Texas A&M application essays from summer 2022 (pre-ChatGPT, obtained by public-records request): "falsely flagged 1% to 2% of the essays as likely written by AI, in some cases claiming to have near 100% certainty"; on 305 summer-2023 essays the same tools flagged ~9%. Tian: "Every AI detector has blind spots" [S45, high].
- **Pangram technical report, arXiv 2402.14873, Feb 2024 (competitor)** — on 1,976 docs across 10 domains and 8 LLMs, GPTZero's FPR ~2%, FNR 10.02%; on GPT-4/GPT-4-Turbo output GPTZero and Originality "completely fail"; on Enron emails GPTZero 89% accuracy; on the TOEFL set the updated GPTZero model "still achieves a false positive rate of 7.7%, or 1.1% if 'Possible AI Content' is generously labeled a negative" [S24, high; competitor-run].
- **RAID, Dugan et al., ACL 2024** — accuracy at 5% FPR on non-adversarial text: GPTZero 66.5 total (Originality 85.0, Binoculars 79.6, Winston 71.0, ZeroGPT 65.5). Per generator: GPT-2 38.8, GPT-3 70.1, ChatGPT 99.4, GPT-4 97.1, Cohere 43.9, Mistral 28.9, Mistral-chat 95.6, MPT 24.8, MPT-chat 85.9, LLaMA-chat 98.5. Under attack: paraphrase 64.0, synonym 61.0, misspelling 65.1, homoglyph 66.2, whitespace 66.2, delete-articles 61.0. "All detectors were sensitive to homoglyph attacks except for GPTZero which sustained only a 0.3% loss ... while five others dropped an average of 40.6%"; GPTZero "unusually robust to adversarial attacks." Authors infer training-data bias toward chat models [S15, high].
- **Najjar et al., arXiv 2501.03203, Jan 2025** — 1,000-item CyberHumanAI set; GPTZero reached 48.5% accuracy on a three-class (pure AI / pure human / mixed) task, tending to label short or hard cases as mixed or unrecognised; the authors' XGBoost baseline 83% [S46, high].
- **Schmalz & Tack, BEA workshop (ACL) 2025, KU Leuven** — GPTZero's monthly AI Vocabulary lists (Oct 2024-Mar 2025; only three distinct lists were actually published, with duplicates/errors) as Bag-of-Words features on the Ghostbuster student-essay corpus: "classification results were close to random, with accuracy ranging from 0.363 to 0.755"; best case (ChatGPT subset, Bernoulli NB) accuracy 0.755, precision 0.882, recall 0.588, AUROC 0.595; of 245 listed terms only 98 occurred at all in the corpus; term presence beats frequency; lists "significantly underperform" plain BoW classifiers [S47, high].
- **Dik, Erdem & Dik, arXiv 2506.23517, June 2025** — 78 essays (28 AI, 50 human) at three lengths: AI essays scored 91-100% AI; "a handful" of human essays were false positives with fluctuating scores; "Educators should therefore exercise caution when relying solely on AI detection tools" [S48, high].
- **Jabarian & Imas, Chicago Booth / NBER WP 34223, Aug-Sept 2025** — 1,992 pre-2020 human passages across six genres paired with GPT-4.1, Claude Opus 4, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.0 Flash outputs; GPTZero scored via `average_generated_prob`. GPTZero FPR 0.0071 at every threshold 0.1-0.9 (Pangram 0.000-0.001; Originality 0.001-0.003); FNR 0.002-0.030 by model; AUROC 0.993 overall (0.980 reviews - 0.999 novels); stubs (<50 words) AUROC 0.7778-1.0 with "substantially higher miss rate"; after StealthGPT "humanizing," "GPTZero largely loses its capacity to detect AI-generated text, with FNR scores around 0.50 and above"; cost per true positive $0.0575 vs Pangram $0.0228; "minimizing FPR favors GPTZero, while maximizing AUROC favors OriginalityAI"; neither "suitable for very short text." Only Pangram met a strict FPR cap of 0.005 [S16, high].
- **Van Vlasselaer, Van Droogenbroeck & Spruyt, IJEI, 29 June 2026 (VUB)** — 160 ground-truth papers (human / fully AI / hybrid / humanised) across GPTZero, Pangram, Copyleaks, Turnitin: "All tools correctly identified fully human texts"; the non-Pangram tools "significantly underestimated GenAI content, particularly for texts generated with the most advanced model"; detectors "should not be used as sole evidence in high-stakes decision-making." Secondary summaries report GPTZero called 70% of fully-AI papers human and underestimated hybrids (ground-truth median 44.6% AI); a correction notice was issued 6 Oct 2026 [S49, medium — full text behind a JS gate; abstract read on VUB portal].
- **Epoch AI, 15 July 2026** — Pangram 3.3.2, GPTZero (2026-05-11-base), Originality Turbo 3.0.2 on 495 human passages (99 authors; blog/fiction/science) and 297 AI passages per condition from Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro. GPTZero: 0/495 false positives (90% CI 0-0.54%); basic-prompt misses 2/297 (0.67%); author-imitation misses 32/297 (10.77%; CI 8.16-14.10%) — blogging 6/99, fiction 2/99, scientific 24/99; "Mixed" verdicts on wholly-AI text counted as misses (8.8% if counted as hits) [S17, high].
- **Karr, Khvatskii, Hua & Chawla, arXiv 2608.11256, Aug 2026** — with an unnamed commercial detector: unmodified 2023-25 published abstracts flagged at 9-15%; "refine abstract only" light edits flagged at 38-80%; elevated scores tracked "long words and academic vocabulary density rather than authorship"; after a humanizer <4% of AI rewrites were flagged; "detector scores should not be used as standalone evidence of misconduct" [S50, high; detector identity not confirmed].
- **Giray, Sevnarayan & Maphoto, Assessing Writing, 2026** — review/critique: GPTZero is not reliable enough "as a definitive tool for high-stakes authorship decisions"; multilingual/L2 writers' "carefully built English can receive high AI-likelihood scores" [S51, medium — abstract only].
- **Journalism**: Ars Technica (Jul 2023) Constitution test [S18]; Bloomberg (Oct 2024) 500-essay test [S45]; Wikipedia cites a 2023 Futurism test concluding teachers "would end up falsely accusing nearly 20 percent of [innocent students]" and a Sept 2025 Business Insider eight-detector/500-document test naming GPTZero "best free AI detector" [S52, low — neither original located].

## Documented failures and false positives

- **US Constitution / Bible (Apr-Jul 2023).** A viral April 2023 screenshot showed GPTZero rating a passage of the Constitution "likely to be written entirely by AI"; Ars Technica reproduced it and got the same on Bible selections; ZeroGPT agreed, OpenAI's classifier said "unclear." Tian's explanation: "The US Constitution is a text fed repeatedly into the training data of many large language models," and GPTZero "predicts text likely to be generated by large language models" [S18, high via AI Incident Database mirror of the Ars text].
- **UC Davis, spring 2023 (William Quarterman).** A history professor failed Quarterman on a take-home exam and referred him for misconduct, "the charge buttressed by GPTZero"; he was cleared after producing his Google Docs revision history, reporting panic attacks during the process (USA Today via EdSurge, Futurism, Ars) [S20, S53, high]. A UC Davis student told The Aggie that "of the 247 documents [my sister] ran through GPTZero, 40% were falsely detected to have used AI" (anecdotal) [S54, medium].
- **Non-native English writers (2023-).** Liang et al.: TOEFL essays flagged at 61.22% average across detectors including GPTZero, because "essays authored by non-native writers inherently exhibit reduced linguistic variability"; simplifying US 8th-grade essays' word choices raised their misclassification from 5.19% to 56.65% [S19, high]. GPTZero's response claims the bias was fixed by Oct 2023 (1/91 flagged) [S23]; Pangram reads the same output as 7.7% unless "possible AI" is excused [S24]; Weber-Wulff found 6/9 machine-translated texts flagged, warning that "L2 students ... using machine translation to translate their own texts" are at risk [S14, high].
- **Rignol v. Yale (D. Conn., No. 3:25-cv-00159, filed Feb 2025).** Yale SOM Executive-MBA student Thierry Rignol (French national) was flagged after a May 2024 take-home final; Prof. Geert Rouwenhorst's letter said some answers "score high on the likelihood of being AI generated using ChatGPTzero." His 13-count complaint alleges Yale's own policy bars such tools "due to their high false-positive rates, particularly for non-native English speakers." Yale says the Honor Committee "did not rely on the ChatGPTZero scans" and suspended him for a year for not being forthcoming (later also an F). Judge Sarah Russell denied a preliminary injunction on 5 May 2025 (no irreparable harm; merits not reached); a motion to dismiss was pending and discovery stayed as of Nov 2025 / Mar 2026. No court has ruled on GPTZero's reliability [S21, S55, S56, high].
- **Short texts.** NBER: AUROC falls to 0.7778 on some <50-word categories with "substantially higher miss rate"; Najjar et al.: short items pushed to "mixed/unrecognised"; GPTZero itself: accuracy "higher for longer inputs" [S16, S46, S4].
- **"Humanizers" and paraphrase.** NBER: FNR >=0.50 after StealthGPT rewrites [S16]; RAID: paraphrase cost only 2.5 points but synonym/article-deletion cost 5.5 [S15]; GPTZero's own paper concedes Dipper "initially had a high bypass rate" [S6].
- **Author-style imitation.** Epoch AI: 24% of AI scientific-writing passages prompted with five samples of a real author's work went undetected [S17].
- **Fully AI academic papers from frontier models.** VUB 2026: GPTZero "significantly underestimated" AI content in fully-AI and hybrid papers (reported 70% full misses) [S49, medium].
- **Polished/edited human text.** Bloomberg: using Grammarly to "improve" an essay or "make it sound academic" "will turn work that passed as 100% human-written to 100% AI-written" (detector not named for that test); Karr et al.: light LLM edits flagged 38-80% [S45, S50]. GPTZero added a "Polished" class in 2025-26 partly in response [S6].
- **Score inconsistency across surfaces.** GPTZero's own support note (Jan 2024) says the Chrome extension ran an older binary model so its AI percentage differed from the website; 2026 Web Store reviews still report "the extension and browser sometimes gives different results" [S10, S30, medium].
- **AI Vocabulary data quality.** The Nov/Dec 2024 list "contained some errors, such as missing words, duplicate entries, and phrase variations"; no new list Feb-Mar 2025 despite the "updates monthly" label [S47, high].
- **Benchmark dispute.** Chicago Booth used `average_generated_prob`; GPTZero says this was the wrong field and that the authors "did not reach out to GPTZero"; GPTZero's re-run moves it from a "secondary tier" to first place — an unresolved methodological dispute, not an error either side has conceded [S16, S36].
- **Commercial complaints (unverified).** Trustpilot reviews allege unexpected recurring charges and cancellation difficulty [S57, low].

## Linguistic signals (what the research says separates human from LLM prose)

- Low perplexity (predictable word choice) and low burstiness (uniform sentence-level perplexity/length) — the original GPTZero heuristics; also why formal, legal, canonical, and L2 writing get flagged [S3, S18, S19].
- Restricted lexical diversity and simpler word choice: TOEFL essays unanimously flagged had "significantly lower perplexity"; simplifying native essays' vocabulary raised flags from 5.19% to 56.65% [S19].
- Grammatical profile of instruction-tuned LLMs (Reinhart et al., PNAS 2025, Biber features): present-participial clauses "at 2 to 5 times the rate of human text" (GPT-4o ~5x), nominalizations ~1.5-2x, "that"-clauses as subject 2.6-3.3x (GPT-4o), agentless passives about half the human rate, less clausal coordination; "an informationally dense, noun-heavy style"; base Llama models are near-human, instruct models diverge; words like "camaraderie," "palpable," "tapestry," "intricate" >100x human rate; obscenities >100x rarer [S33, high].
- Excess vocabulary in scientific writing (Kobak et al., Science Advances, July 2025): ~900 "excess words" (e.g., "delve," "intricacies") rising sharply after 2023; >=13.5% of 2024 PubMed abstracts estimated LLM-processed [S34, medium].
- Stylometric footprint (Shan, Lee & Hao, EMNLP 2026): AI-*generated* text shows jointly higher lexical diversity and lower entropy across 8 LLMs and 5 domains; AI-*edited* text does not — it shifts mainly in lexical density, and is hard to separate from human writing [S35, high].
- GPTZero's own frequency-ratio lexicon: "play a significant role in shaping" 182x, "today's fast-paced world" 107x, "notable works include" >120x, "showcasing" 20x, "remarked" 18x, "aligns" 16x, "surpassing" 12x, "tragically" 11x, "impacting" >=11x; but on student essays only 98 of 245 listed terms ever appeared [S27, S47].
- GPTZero's ten AI Patterns: inflated importance ("pivotal moment," "enduring legacy"), name-dropping outlets, empty commentary, sales-pitch tone ("nestled in the heart of," "vibrant"), phantom experts, dressed-up verbs ("serves as"), "not X but Y," triplets, unnecessary caveats, chatbot leakage [S25].
- Explainable-AI contrast in educational text: human writing favours "practical" words; AI favours "abstract, formal vocabulary" [S46]. Detector scores on published abstracts rise with "long words and academic vocabulary density" independent of authorship [S50].
- GPTZero 4o claims to be "indifferent to punctuation changes" such as em dashes — i.e., the vendor says punctuation is *not* a load-bearing signal in its model [S8].

## Open questions

1. Per-detector TOEFL false-positive rates in Liang et al. are only in a figure; GPTZero's exact 2023 number is unverified here.
2. GPTZero's "seven indicators" have never been enumerated publicly.
3. The Chicago Booth field dispute (`average_generated_prob` vs the field GPTZero prefers) has not been adjudicated by a third party.
4. The VUB 2026 per-tool figures (70% full misses, hybrid underestimation) come from secondary summaries; the paper has a 6 Oct 2026 correction whose content is unknown.
5. Karr et al. 2026 do not name the commercial detector; attribution to GPTZero is unconfirmed.
6. No independent replication of the "1 in 10,402" PERSUADE claim or the frontier-model recall numbers for GPTZero 4o/4.11b exists yet.
7. Pricing: GPTZero's page is client-rendered; third-party trackers disagree on monthly prices (Essential ~$8.33-15, Premium ~$12.99-24, Professional ~$24.99-46; free tier 10,000 words/month) [S58, low].
