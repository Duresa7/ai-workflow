# Commercial AI-text detectors beyond GPTZero/Turnitin: Originality.ai, Copyleaks, Winston, ZeroGPT, Sapling, QuillBot/Scribbr, Grammarly, Hive, AI or Not, and OpenAI's retired classifier

Research notes compiled 2026-10-13. Confidence tags: **[high]** = read the primary source; **[medium]** = secondary source or vendor summary of a third-party study; **[low]** = unverified/affiliate-site figure. Vendor-published numbers are labelled **(vendor)** throughout.

## Summary

- Every detector in this file is a black-box classifier. None discloses architecture, training data, or decision threshold; the most any vendor says is "a fine-tuned large language model trained on millions of known-AI and known-human records" (Originality) or "a Transformer that estimates the probability each token is AI-generated" (Sapling). [high]
- Vendor accuracy claims cluster at 99%+ with sub-1% false positives (Copyleaks 0.2-0.3%, Originality Lite 0.5%, Winston 99.98%, ZeroGPT 98.4%/<1%, Grammarly "99%"). Independent measurements on clean, unedited, English prose from 2025-2026 are far better than in 2023 (several tools now post 0 false positives on small human sets), but they diverge from vendor claims as soon as text is short, edited, translated, non-native, paraphrased, or hybrid. [high]
- The clearest 2026 finding is from an inter-rater study of nine detectors on 90 academic passages: agreement on pure-human and pure-AI text was 96-97%, but on hybrid texts (one-third AI) mean pairwise Cohen's kappa fell to 0.02 (chance), 28 of 30 hybrids got both a "human" and an "AI" verdict, and 41% of all texts received contradictory verdicts. Copyleaks scored every hybrid 0; Originality scored half of them 0 and half 100; Sapling saturated near 72. [high]
- Independent 2026 false-positive measurements for Originality.ai Turbo 3.0.2 (3.84% on 495 pre-2022 passages, Epoch AI) and for GPTZero's own benchmark of Originality (4.79%) sit well above Originality's 1.5% vendor figure; Pangram and GPTZero posted 0/495 in the same Epoch test. [high]
- Originality.ai's recall on newest OpenAI models was poor in GPTZero's vendor benchmark (GPT-5 31.7%, GPT-5-mini 7.3%, o3 7.6%); Copyleaks 47.5%/18.0%. Vendor-run, but consistent with the pattern that detectors lag new generator releases. [medium]
- ZeroGPT remains the most-cited source of absurd false positives (US Declaration of Independence 97.93% AI, Oct 2024; Texas Declaration 86.54%, May 2025) and could not reach a 5% false-positive operating point on RAID; yet it scores well on some narrow academic corpora (98% on physics essays). [high]
- Grammarly's detector claims "#1 on RAID" (its Feb 2026 self-submission scored 99.47% TPR at 5% FPR), but in Pangram's Jan 2026 free-tool test it flagged 0 of 9 AI texts and in a March 2026 peer-reviewed obfuscation study it had the lowest baseline (70.6% mean) and fell to 19.0% on paraphrased Grok text. Leaderboard scores and real-use behaviour are not the same thing. [high]
- Copyleaks is the most consistently strong tool across independent tests on raw AI text (100% in JAIT 2026; 9/9 in Pangram's test; top-3 in Walters 2023; "most accurate" in the 2023 Cornell-hosted study) but is conservative on partial AI (0% on hybrids in TextPulse; 0% "fully AI" classification on 40 fully AI papers in Van Vlasselaer 2026 per Pangram's summary). Bloomberg measured 1-2% false positives for Copyleaks and GPTZero on 500 pre-ChatGPT admissions essays. [high]
- OpenAI's own classifier (Jan 31 2023) caught 26% of AI text and mislabelled 9% of human text; OpenAI withdrew it on July 20 2023 "due to its low rate of accuracy." [high]
- Documented harms: freelance writers fired on Originality/Copyleaks flags (Gizmodo 2024); students zeroed on Turnitin/GPTZero flags (Bloomberg 2024, NPR 2025); Newby v. Adelphi (NY, Jan 2026) annulled an integrity finding that rested on a single Turnitin score after two other detectors called the essay human. [high]
- Linguistic signals that research says separate LLM from human prose: low perplexity / low lexical and syntactic variability (hits non-native writers), excess "style" vocabulary (delves, underscores, showcasing, intricate), grammatical tics (present-participial clauses 5.3x human rate, nominalizations 2.1x, far fewer agentless passives), near-absence of typos, uniform sentence rhythm, and simpler readability. Detectors trained on these cues flag formulaic, edited, translated, or polished human writing. [high]

## How it works / Mechanics

### Common architecture (all vendors)
- Three families: (1) statistical scoring against an LM's probability distribution (perplexity; sentence-level variance of surprisal, "burstiness"); (2) supervised classifiers trained on labelled human/AI corpora; (3) watermarking (requires generator cooperation; absent from all products here). "Commercial vendors do not disclose their architectures, training data, or decision thresholds." (TextPulse 2026, s3.1) [high]
- Outputs are not on a common scale: some return P(AI), some "% of text that is AI," some a label. Each vendor picks its own cutoff. [high]
- RAID (ACL 2024) found closed-source detectors are "calibrated fairly well, with none having an FPR above 1.7%" at naive thresholds, but all are "easily fooled" by repetition penalty (up to -32 points), random sampling vs greedy decoding, and unseen generators. [high]

### Originality.ai
- Vendor description: "supervised learning of a carefully fine-tuned large AI language model," trained on "millions of carefully selected records of known AI and known human content"; architecture undisclosed. [high]
- Model lineage (vendor page, release history): 1.1 beta Nov 2022 (could be "tricked" by paraphrasing); 1.4 Apr 2023; 2.0 Standard Aug 2023 (retired Sept 2024); **3.0 Turbo Feb 2024** (trained on Grok, Mixtral, GPT-4 Turbo, Gemini, Claude 2; "90.2% -> 98.8%" on toughest set, FP 2.9%->2.8%); **1.0.0 Lite July 2024** (98%, <1% FP; positioned for lower false positives on web/marketing content); 3.0.1 Turbo Oct 2024 (99%+, <3% FP); Multilingual 2.0.0 May 2025 (97.8% over 30 languages, 2.4% FP); 1.0.1/1.0.2 Lite Jun/Sep 2025 (99%, 0.5% FP); **3.0.2 Turbo Sep 2025** (99%+, 1.5% FP, "up to 97% on latest humanizers"); 0.0.5 Academic Sep 2025 (99%+, <1% FP); **"AI Allowance" mid-2026** (spectrum scoring; 99.4% accuracy at a 15% allowance on a 456,872-sample internal "V6" benchmark covering GPT-5, Claude 4.5, Gemini 2.0). [high, all vendor]
- Vendor notes ROC/AUROC "considered but not used" because competitors' sensitivity cannot be adjusted; reports confusion matrix, TPR, TNR, accuracy, F1. [high, vendor]
- Score semantics: the "70% Original / 30% AI" output is a confidence score, not a share of AI text (Gizmodo, confirmed by CEO). [high]

### Copyleaks (AI Content Detector, API, Chrome/Edge extension)
- Vendor description: returns per-section human/AI classification plus overall summary; "AI Logic" shows phrase-level reasons; three "levels" (verbatim AI; small edits; heavily edited); 30+ languages; detects ChatGPT, GPT-4, Gemini, Claude, DeepSeek, Llama. Minimum input 350 characters. Since v7.1 three sensitivity settings: Extra Safe, Balanced (default), Extra Sensitive. [high, vendor]
- Training claim: "analyzed trillions of crawled and user-sourced content pages to train its models to understand the patterns of human writing." [medium, store listing]
- Chrome extension v5.0.1 (updated Sept 28 2026, ~200,000 users, 4.1/5 from 708 ratings): highlight text on any page, click icon; pitched for "assignments and writings, social media posts, news articles" and shopping reviews; claims "Over 99% accuracy and a 0.2% false positive rate." [high]

### Winston AI
- No public description of method beyond "detection model built with diverse types of content generated by all known Large Language Models," "updated on a weekly basis," sentence-level highlighting, "specifically trained on paraphrased and humanized content." Model names: Luka (3.0, 2023), Curia (4.0), Winston 5.0 (2026). [high, vendor]
- Vendor caveats: reliability drops under ~300 words; formal legal/scientific/standardized-test prose more prone to false positives. [high, vendor]

### ZeroGPT
- Vendor: "DeepAnalyse(TM) Technology," "multi-stage methodology," trained on "extensive text collections from the internet, educational datasets" plus synthetic data; "35+ LLM families" including "GPT 5, GPT 6"; "all languages"; 15,000-character limit. Claims 98.4% accuracy, <1% FP, no benchmark given. [high, vendor]

### Sapling
- Vendor: "a machine learning system (a Transformer) similar to that used to generate AI content"; instead of generating, "estimates the probability that each token is AI-generated"; retrained on ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi; "much more accurate after 50 or so words"; free tier checks first 2,000 characters; English + 7 languages (Italian/Dutch have "no training data yet"); other languages scored by the English model. Vendor explicitly says no detector "should be used as a standalone check." Internal claim: >97% detection, <3% FP; "false positives fell by 60%" on held-out eval (no baseline given). [high, vendor]

### QuillBot AI Detector and Scribbr AI Detector (same engine)
- Scribbr's detector is a "wrap-around" of QuillBot's; both owned by Learneo (formerly Course Hero). Identical outputs reported by reviewers. [medium, GPTZero review + multiple reviewers]
- QuillBot (launched July 2024): "custom model architecture," sentence-level analysis, flags "AI-generated," "AI-refined/paraphrased," "human"; low/moderate/high confidence; free tier 1,200 words. QuillBot's public explanation of signals: "AI-generated content is likely to contain repetitive words, awkward phrasing, and an unnatural, choppy flow." [medium; Bloomberg quote high]
- QuillBot self-submitted to the RAID leaderboard (Jan 30 2025): 99.6% TPR at 5% FPR, AUROC 0.996. [high, vendor-run on public benchmark]

### Grammarly AI Detector
- Vendor: "uses machine learning models to recognize patterns that distinguish AI-generated content from human writing"; "segments the text and analyzes each section"; returns a percentage; "trained on tens of thousands of texts"; "Designed to avoid wrongly flagging human-written text." Claims "99% detection accuracy and ranks #1 on RAID's independent benchmark." Also states "there is no AI detector that can conclusively or definitively determine whether AI was used." Companion "Authorship" feature tracks typed vs pasted vs AI text. [high, vendor]
- RAID self-submission (Feb 9 2026): 99.47% TPR at 5% FPR, 98.2% at 1% FPR, AUROC 0.9987. Note: RAID's leaderboard is self-submitted predictions on a hidden test set whose public training split is available to vendors; by Sept 24 2026 Winston AI's submission (99.98% at 5% FPR) outranked it. [high]

### Hive Moderation (AI-Generated Text Detection API)
- Docs: single `ai_generated` head returning one classification with 0.0-1.0 confidence; text split into 2,048-character chunks each scored, for highlighting; "trained on a vast dataset of both human-written and artificially generated text from many genres," with emphasis on "essays and school assignments"; intended for schools and for platforms enforcing bans on AI text. No languages, minimum length, or accuracy stated in docs. [high]
- Launch post (Feb 1 2023, updated Jul 2025): 242-passage test set incl. ESL writing; "balanced accuracy stands at an impressive 99%" vs GPTZero 83%, OpenAI 73%; claimed FP 1% vs OpenAI 12.5%, GPTZero 30%, Writer 46%. [high, vendor]
- The widely quoted "98.03% accuracy / 0% FP" University of Chicago figure is for Hive's image detector, not text. [medium]

### AI or Not
- API: `POST /v2/text/sync`; input 250-500,000 characters (~64-word minimum); returns `report.ai_text.confidence` (0-1), `is_detected`, optional block-level annotations. No method, languages, threshold, or text-specific accuracy documented. Homepage claims "98.9% accuracy" across images, text, video, deepfakes (not text-specific). No independent evaluation of its text detector was found. [high for docs; absence of evals: high]

### OpenAI AI Text Classifier (retired)
- "a language model fine-tuned on a dataset of pairs of human-written text and AI-written text on the same topic" (human side from pretraining data and InstructGPT demonstrations; AI side from 34 models of five organizations). Labels from "very unlikely" to "likely." "very unreliable on short texts (below 1,000 characters)"; English only; "Sometimes human-written text will be incorrectly but confidently labeled as AI-written"; "For inputs that are very different from text in our training set, the classifier is sometimes extremely confident in a wrong prediction." [high, archived primary]

## Measured accuracy and independent evaluations

### Large shared benchmarks
- **RAID (Dugan et al., ACL 2024; 6.3M generations, 11 generators, 8 domains, 11 attacks).** Accuracy at 5% FPR, non-adversarial (Table 5 extraction, ordering by generator/decoding partially ambiguous): Originality 51-99.9 (strong on ChatGPT ~98.2% per vendor restatement, weak on repetition-penalty sampling); GPTZero 4.8-98.8; Winston 11.3-97.2; ZeroGPT 0.3-95.4 and "could not reach the target FPR" (plateau 16.9%). Under attacks (Table 6): Originality None 85.0 / Paraphrase 96.7 / Synonym 96.5 / Misspelling 78.6 / **Homoglyph 9.3** / Whitespace 84.9 / Delete-articles 71.4; GPTZero 61-66.5 across all. RAID-extra: Originality Code 8.5%, Czech 69.8%, German 89.1%; Winston Code 24.5/Czech 70.3/German 73.8; ZeroGPT 13.8/49.3/51.7. [high]
- **RAID leaderboard self-submissions (2025-2026).** TPR at 5% FPR: Winston AI 99.98% (Sept 24 2026); Grammarly 99.47% (Feb 9 2026); QuillBot 99.6% (Jan 30 2025); GPTZero 97.2% (Oct 7 2025). Vendor-run on a public benchmark with a public training split; treat as upper bounds. [high]

### Peer-reviewed / independent multi-tool studies, chronological
- **Weber-Wulff et al. 2023 (IJEI; 14 tools incl. Winston, ZeroGPT, GPTZero, OpenAI, Turnitin, Writer; 54 docs).** "all scored below 80% of accuracy and only 5 over 70%"; binary accuracy Winston 67%, ZeroGPT 59%, OpenAI 54%, GPTZero 54%; false-accusation ratio 0% for Winston, ZeroGPT, OpenAI; 50% for GPTZero; machine translation cut accuracy on human text by 20 points; machine-paraphrased AI text detected only 26%; tools "biased towards classifying the output as human-written." Copyleaks, Originality, Sapling were not in this study. [high]
- **Liang et al. 2023 (Patterns; 7 detectors incl. Originality, Sapling, ZeroGPT, GPTZero, OpenAI, Crossplag, Quil).** 91 TOEFL essays: mean FPR 61.3%; 19.8% flagged by all seven; 97.8% flagged by at least one; US 8th-grade essays near-perfect. Flagged essays had "significantly lower text perplexity." Prompting ChatGPT to "enhance the word choices" cut FPR to 11.6%. [high]
- **Orenstrakh et al. 2023 (arXiv 2307.07411, cited by Copyleaks as "Cornell").** 124 pre-ChatGPT CS student submissions + 40 ChatGPT; 8 detectors: "CopyLeaks is the most accurate," GPTKit fewest FPs, GPTZero 52 FPs of 114 human docs. Copyleaks' "99.12%/0.2% FP" restatement does not reconcile with the published counts. [high for abstract; medium for Copyleaks' numbers]
- **Walters 2023 (Open Information Science; 16 detectors; 42 GPT-3.5, 42 GPT-4, 42 human essays).** "Copyleaks, TurnItIn, and Originality.ai have high accuracy with all three sets"; most others "generally ineffective at distinguishing between GPT-4 papers and those written by undergraduate students." [high, abstract]
- **Physics essays (arXiv 2403.05458, Mar 2024; 150 human + 150 GPT-4).** ZeroGPT 0 FP/1 FN, acc 0.98; QuillBot 10 FP, acc 0.83; Hive 4 FP/0 FN, acc 0.93; Sapling 18 FP/0 FN, acc 0.70; Radar 2 FP/5 FN, 0.88. [high]
- **Perkins et al. 2024 (IJETHE; 6 detectors incl. Turnitin, Copyleaks, GPTZero, ZeroGPT).** Mean baseline accuracy 39.5%, 17.4% after adversarial edits; secondary write-ups give Copyleaks 73.9% -> 58.7%, Turnitin 50% -> 7.9%, Copyleaks FP 50% on their set. "cannot currently be recommended for determining whether violations of academic integrity have occurred." [high abstract; medium per-tool]
- **Paustian & Slinger 2024 (Frontiers in Education; 459 microbiology responses).** Human vs AI accuracy: Originality 0.91, ZeroGPT 0.91, GPTZero 0.87, Winston 0.80. False positives: Originality 27/153 (17.6%), ZeroGPT 15/153 (9.8%), GPTZero ~24/152, Winston "nearly half." Disguising cut recall 20.6 points. [high]
- **Howard et al. 2024 (JCO CCI; 100 pre-LLM ASCO abstracts + 200 synthetic).** Specificity: GPTZero 100%, Originality 100%, Sapling 99% (1 FP); sensitivity 99.5/96/97%; mixed human/AI AUROC only 0.60-0.71. [high]
- **Bloomberg Businessweek (Oct 18 2024).** 500 Texas A&M application essays from summer 2022 (public-records request, pre-ChatGPT): GPTZero and Copyleaks "falsely flagged 1% to 2% of the essays as likely written by AI, in some cases claiming to have near 100% certainty." A separate 305-essay post-ChatGPT set: ~9% flagged. Grammarly's "improve"/"make it sound academic" rewrites turned 100%-human verdicts into 100%-AI. [high]
- **Scribbr 12-tool test (table titled "Best AI detectors in 2024"; page republished Apr 2026, modified Jul 21 2026).** 30 texts of 1,000-1,500 chars (5 human, 5 GPT-3.5, 5 GPT-4, 5 mixed, 5 QuillBot-paraphrased AI, 5 paraphrased human; last excluded from score). Scribbr premium 84% (0 FP); QuillBot 78% (0); Scribbr free 78% (0); Originality 76% (1 FP); Sapling 68% (0); Copyleaks 66% (0); ZeroGPT 64% (1); GPT-2 Output Detector 58%; CrossPlag 58%; GPTZero 52% (1); Writer 38%; OpenAI 38% (1). Scribbr sells one of the tools. [high, vendor-adjacent]
- **PCWorld (Nov 25 2024; one Gemini story, two runs each).** Grammarly 37% AI, GPTZero 62%, QuillBot 78%, Originality 100%. [high]
- **Decrypt (Oct 14 2024).** Declaration of Independence preamble: ZeroGPT 97.93% AI; GPTZero 89% human; QuillBot "Human-written 100%"; Grammarly human. Forster's 1909 "The Machine Stops": ZeroGPT called the ChatGPT-4o rewrite human (6.35%); QuillBot 0% on both. [high]
- **Russell, Karpinska & Iyyer (ACL 2025; 300 articles; GPT-4o, Claude, o1-Pro, paraphrased, humanized).** Expert majority vote 99.3% TPR / 0% FPR; Pangram 98.0 (2.0 FPR); GPTZero 85.3 (0.7) but 46.7% on humanized o1-Pro; Binoculars 66.7; RADAR 15.3. [high]
- **Alshammari & Rao (arXiv 2507.17944, Jul 2025; DeepSeek-v3).** QuillBot and Copyleaks "near-perfect" on raw and paraphrased; on humanized text Copyleaks 71%, QuillBot 58%, GPTZero 52%. [high]
- **Erol et al. 2025 (Acta Neurochirurgica).** 250 human + 750 ChatGPT texts; Corrector, ZeroGPT, GPTZero; 40 (16%) human articles scored >50% by ZeroGPT (secondary reading). [medium]
- **PeerJ CS 2025 "accuracy-bias trade-offs" (72 pre-LLM abstracts, half non-native authors), as extended by Originality.** GPTZero 98.15% / 2.78% FN; ZeroGPT 64.35% / 45.14% FN; DetectGPT 54.63%; Originality Lite 98.61%, Turbo 97.69%; on non-native authors Lite/Turbo 2.78% FP. [medium, vendor extension of peer-reviewed data]
- **Kaya Ergen 2025 (Beyoglu Eye J; 50 human + 50 GPT-4o ophthalmology intros).** GPTZero AUROC 1.000 (sens 100/spec 96); Writer 0.981; ZeroGPT 0.836 (sens 38%/spec 94% at optimal cutoff); QuillBot paraphrase dropped GPTZero median 100% -> 23%. [high]
- **JAAD International 2025 (letter; GPTZero and Originality.AI).** Original 2020/2024 research letters: 97-100% classified human. After one ChatGPT-4o "enhance readability and flow" pass: 75-85% flagged AI, 15-25% at high confidence; Originality similar. [high]

Recall by generator: GPT-5 100/47.5/31.7; GPT-5-mini 94.9/18.0/7.3; o3 96.3/45.4/7.6; GPT-4o 99.1/73.4/97.7; Claude Sonnet 4 99.0/74.0/95.1; Gemini-2.5-pro 95.8/59.7/78.2. [high, vendor]
- **Almohaimeed et al. (arXiv 2511.16690, Nov 2025; Arabic).** 800 articles + 16,400 polished samples; Originality.ai best commercial tool at 92% (96% in a second cut) with 8% FP, but accuracy "dropped to 12%" on human articles lightly polished by Mistral or Gemma-3; Claude-4 Sonnet as judge 83.5% -> 57.6%. FNR basic prompt: 0.00 / 0.67 / 0.34%. FNR five-shot style imitation: 10.10 / 10.77 / 17.85%; scientific genre hardest (25/24/29%). [high]

Reported figures (Originality 89.4%/9.4% FP; Copyleaks 88.1%/11.2%; ZeroGPT 82.3%/16.8%; Sapling 78.9%/19.4%; Winston 74.2%/24.4%; Scribbr 66.8%/31.6%) should not be cited. [low]

### Per-vendor independent picture (condensed)

## Linguistic signals research says separate human from LLM prose

- **Predictability / perplexity and lexical variability.** Most detectors score how predictable each next word is; text with "more limited range of linguistic expressions" (non-native writers, formulaic or neurodivergent styles) has lower perplexity and is flagged (Liang 2023; Zou in Bloomberg: "If the word choices tend to be more generic and formulaic, that work has a higher chance of being flagged"). [high]
- **Burstiness.** GPTZero's stated signal: variance of perplexity across a document; "people tend to vary their sentence construction and diction a lot throughout a document" (Bloomberg). Reinhart et al. (PNAS 2025): "LLMs struggle to match human stylistic variation"; instruction tuning widens the gap. [high]
- **Grammatical profile (Reinhart et al., PNAS 2025; Biber features; GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Llama 3 8B/70B base and instruct vs human corpora).** GPT-4o uses present participial clauses 5.3x the human rate (d=1.38), nominalizations 2.1x (d=1.23), that-clauses as subjects 2.6x, phrasal coordination 1.9x, agentless passives ~0.5x; instruction-tuned models push toward "an informationally dense, noun-heavy style"; words like camaraderie, palpable, tapestry at >100x human rate; feature-based random forest separated 7 sources at 66% (chance 14%), single-model classifiers 93-98%, base Llama hardest (~75%). [high]
- **Excess vocabulary (Kobak et al., Science Advances 2025; 15M PubMed abstracts).** 2024 excess-use ratios: delves 28.0x, underscores 13.8x, showcasing 10.7x; common-word gaps: potential, findings, crucial; 2024 excess words were almost entirely "style" words (66% verbs, 14% adjectives) vs content words in the Covid era; implied >=13.5% of 2024 abstracts LLM-processed (up to ~40% in some subcorpora; China/Korea/Taiwan ~0.20 vs UK/Australia ~0.05; MDPI/Frontiers/Cureus ~0.20-0.25 vs Nature/Science/Cell 0.07). [high]
- **Focal words (Juzek & Ward, COLING 2025; 26.7M PubMed abstracts).** 21 focal words overused by ChatGPT-3.5 and spiking 2020->2024, e.g. delves +6,697%, delved +2,240%, delving +1,817%, showcasing +1,396%, delve +1,375%, boasts +918%, underscores +904%, comprehending +899%, intricacies +773%, surpassing +667%, intricate +611%, underscoring +537%. No evidence the cause is architecture, algorithms, or training data; model testing "consistent with RLHF" contributing. [high]
- **What expert human detectors use (Russell et al., ACL 2025).** Share of explanations citing each cue: vocabulary 53.1%, sentence structure 35.9%, grammar & punctuation 24.8% (AI text is too clean), originality 23.7%, quotes 22.3%, clarity 19.5%; example "AI vocabulary": vibrant, crucial, significantly, testament; "delve" and "crucial" in human text trigger human false positives too; formality cited for o1-Pro. Expert majority vote beat every detector incl. on humanized text. [high]
- **Academic register density.** Karr et al. 2026: detector scores track long-token and Academic Word List density rather than authorship; non-STEM abstracts flagged more. [high]
- **Readability.** Hocenski et al. 2026: human texts "more complex and suited to highly educated readers," AI texts "simpler in structure." [high]
- **Decoding artefacts.** RAID: greedy decoding is far easier to detect than random sampling; adding a repetition penalty cuts detector accuracy by up to 32 points; homoglyph/zero-width characters break Originality. [high]
- **Vendor-stated cues.** QuillBot: "repetitive words, awkward phrasing, and an unnatural, choppy flow"; Copyleaks "AI Logic" phrase-level explanations; Winston: formal legal/scientific/standardized-test prose prone to false positives. [high]
- **Caveat.** arXiv 2603.23146 shows the discriminative features are dataset-specific; the Reinhart/Kobak/Juzek signals are population-level tendencies, not per-document proof. Human writers who are terse, formal, non-native, or professionally edited share many of these features. [high]
