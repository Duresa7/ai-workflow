# Turnitin's AI writing detector: mechanics, claimed vs. measured accuracy, failures (research notes through 2026-10-13)

Compiled 2026-10-13. "Vendor" = Turnitin's own claims (press releases, blog, help center). "Independent" = peer-reviewed or institutional tests not run by Turnitin. Confidence tags on sources: high = primary source read directly; medium = read via a secondary/republished source; low = unverified.

## Summary

- Turnitin's detector (launched 4 April 2023, preview since Feb 2023) is a transformer classifier trained on Turnitin's in-house corpus of academic writing. It splits a submission into overlapping segments of prose sentences, scores each segment 0-1, lets each sentence inherit (pooled) segment scores, and aggregates to a document-level "percentage of qualifying text likely AI-generated." Only long-form prose counts; lists, tables, code, poetry and bibliographies are excluded. Minimum 300 words (raised from 150 in May 2023), maximum 30,000 words. [S3][S5][S9]
- Vendor accuracy claims: Feb 2023 "identifies 97 percent of ChatGPT and GPT3 authored writing, with a very low less than 1/100 false positive rate"; April 2023 "we only flag something as AI-written when we are 98% sure"; and since mid-2023 "false positive rate under 1% for documents with over 20% of AI writing." Turnitin says it re-validates this on "over 700,000" pre-ChatGPT papers before each model release (800,000 in 2023). Turnitin has never published a document-level false-positive rate for the 0-19% band. [S1][S2][S4][S5]
- The retreat: on 23 May 2023, seven weeks after launch, CPO Annie Chechitelli wrote that "real-world use is yielding different results from our lab," disclosed a sentence-level false-positive rate of "~4%," admitted a "higher incidence of false positives" below 20%, added an asterisk to sub-20% scores, raised the word minimum to 300, and changed how introductions/conclusions are aggregated. [S4][S6][S7]
- The 20% threshold: since 16 July 2024 scores of 1-19% are not shown at all (asterisk only, no highlights); Turnitin's stated reason is "to avoid potential incidence of false positives." In May 2023 Turnitin had considered hiding low scores but said educators "did not like the idea of hidden AI percentage scores." [S4][S9]
- Independent evaluations consistently find Turnitin among the best detectors on unmodified AI text with very low false positives on clean human text (Weber-Wulff 2023: 0 of 18 human/translated docs flagged, 76% overall accuracy, ranked 1 of 14; Perkins 2024: 0 false accusations on 10 human controls), but accuracy collapses on paraphrased, "humanized," hybrid or newer-model text (Perkins 2024: 50% to 7.9% after adversarial edits, the largest drop of 7 tools; Temple 2023: "no relationship at all" between flagged sentences and the actually AI-written parts of hybrid texts; VUB 2026: 0% detection of fully AI-written papers from the most advanced model, per vendor-relayed table). [S14][S15][S16][S17]
- Washington Post's April 2023 test of 16 samples found Turnitin got "over half" at least partly wrong, including flagging a fully human essay by student Lucy Goetz. [S8][S19]
- Bias: Turnitin was not among the 7 detectors in the Stanford/Liang et al. 2023 study (61% of TOEFL essays flagged). Turnitin's own Oct 2023 evaluation (vendor) reported no statistically significant FPR difference between L1 and L2 writers for documents of 300+ words, but higher and significantly different FPRs for 150-300-word texts. A 2026 EMNLP study of 135,389 non-native/edited manuscript pairs found detector FPRs on human text "ranged from 0.0% to 100.0%" and identified editing style as a confound (Turnitin not named). [S10][S11][S12][S13]
- Institutions that disabled or never enabled it include Vanderbilt (Aug 2023, "750 papers" arithmetic, opacity, bias), UCLA (Apr 2023), Cambridge and Durham (2023), Michigan State and Northwestern (2023, per Bloomberg/IHE), UT Austin (no contract; procurement barred for IP/FERPA reasons), Waterloo (Sept 2025, internal test flagged human text as "100% generated by AI"), Australian Catholic University (Mar 2025, after ~6,000 misconduct referrals in 2024), Curtin (1 Jan 2026). Arcadia renewed in June 2026 then did not renew "due to updated terms in the TurnItIn AI contract." [S20]-[S31]
- 2024-2026 product changes: AI paraphrasing detection (Dec 2023 silent; two-color report July 2024), Spanish (Sept 2024), Japanese (Apr 2025), Clarity process-tracking workspace (announced Mar 2025, GA Q3 2025), AI bypasser/"humanizer" detection (27 Aug 2025), model updates "to improve recall" (14 Oct 2025, 12 Feb 2026), consolidation of a multi-model ensemble into a single model (July 2026), removal of the purple "AI-paraphrased" category (4 Aug 2026), Arabic (18 Aug 2026), Turkish/German/Dutch (6 Oct 2026). The English FAQ now lists coverage of GPT-5.x, Gemini 3.x, Claude 4.x, Llama 4, DeepSeek, Grok, Mistral. [S3][S5][S32][S33][S34][S35]
- Turnitin's own usage data: 3.3% of English submissions had 80%+ AI writing in Apr-Aug 2023; 14.8% between Oct 2025 and Feb 2026 (on the newer model, so not strictly comparable). [S36]
- Litigation/press: Newby v. Adelphi (Turnitin flagged essay as fully AI; two other detectors disagreed; court ruled the finding "without merit," Feb 2026); Yang v. University of Minnesota (GPTZero not Turnitin; dismissed Oct 2025, affirmed Feb 2026); Doe v. University of Michigan (Feb 2026, disability discrimination, no detector named); Kato v. Palo Alto USD (May 2026, Turnitin 76%); Leigh Burrell (NYT, May 2025, Turnitin flag despite Google Docs history); Kelsey Auman (Buffalo, May 2025, 1,000+ signature petition); ACU paramedic student (ABC, Oct 2025, 84% report was the sole evidence). [S37]-[S44]
- Turnitin's standing disclaimer, repeated in every help page since 2023: the model "may not always be accurate ... so it should not be used as the sole basis for adverse actions against a student." Several documented cases (ACU, Adelphi, Palo Alto) show institutions doing exactly that. [S9][S41][S42]

## How it works / Mechanics

**Architecture and training (vendor description).** The current English FAQ (updated 13 Oct 2026) says: "Our current system uses a modern deep learning approach based on the transformer architecture ... our models are also trained on our vast inhouse corpus of academic data." It explicitly disclaims hand-crafted signals: "Our model is not explicitly programmed to evaluate specific signals such as 'burstiness,' 'perplexity,' or other individual metrics ... its outputs are generated by many learned patterns working together rather than by a small set of transparent, human-readable rules. For that reason, individual predictions may not always be explainable in simple feature-by-feature terms." [S5] The 2023 FAQ framed the underlying signal as next-word probability: LLMs "generate the next word in a sequence of words in a consistent and highly probable fashion. Human writing, on the other hand, tends to be inconsistent and idiosyncratic." [S3] Training data is described as "a representative sample of data ... that includes both AI generated and authentic academic writing across geographies and subject areas," with deliberate inclusion of "second-language learners, English users from non-English speaking countries ... and less common subject areas such as anthropology, geology, sociology." [S3][S5] Feb 2023 press release: "trained on the largest dataset of academic writing" (Chechitelli) and "using recent advances in deep learning transformer architecture" (Eric Wang, VP AI). [S1] July 2026: "we updated our model architecture to consolidate a multi-model ensemble into a single model." [S5]

**Segmentation and sentence scoring.** 2023 FAQ and Sept 2024 blog: "the submission is first broken into segments of text that are roughly a few hundred words (about five to ten sentences). Those segments are then overlapped with each other to capture each sentence in context ... we give each sentence a score between 0 and 1 ... If it determines the entirety of the sentence was generated by AI it will receive a score of 1." [S3][S45] 2026 FAQ wording: "Each segment is classified by the AI detection model and given a value between 0 and 1 ... Each qualifying sentence within these segments inherits the segment's score. Since segments overlap, some sentences may have multiple scores, which are then pooled into a single score. These sentence scores are further aggregated and used to compute the overall document AI writing score." [S5] Consequence acknowledged by Turnitin: "In shorter documents where there are only a few hundred words, the prediction will be mostly 'all or nothing' because we're predicting on a single segment without the opportunity to overlap." [S3][S5]

**Overall percentage and "qualifying text."** The percentage is the share of *qualifying text* (prose sentences in long-form writing) predicted AI, not the share of the whole document. "The model does not reliably detect AI-generated text in the form of non-prose, such as poetry, scripts, or code, nor does it detect short-form/unconventional writing such as bullet points, tables, or annotated bibliographies." Bibliographies were excluded after an Aug 2023 bug fix that "was occasionally highlighting AI writing within references." Tables became processable in Aug 2023. [S9][S32]

**The 98% confidence framing (2023).** "Using the average scores of all the segments ... the model then generates an overall prediction of how much text (with 98% confidence based on data that was collected and verified in our AI Innovation lab) ... when we say that 40% of the overall text has been AI-generated, we're 98% confident that is the case." And: "We only flag something as AI-written when we are 98% sure ... This means, however, that we will likely miss up to 15% of text written by AI, with a less than 1% false positive rate ... if we identify that 50% of a document is written by AI ... it could contain as much as 65% AI writing." [S3] The 2026 FAQ keeps the 50%/65% example but drops the "98%" language. [S5]

**File requirements (2026).** <100 MB; at least 300 words of prose; not more than 30,000 words (raised from an earlier cap on 16 July 2024); .docx/.pdf/.txt/.rtf; supported languages English, Spanish, Japanese, Arabic, Dutch, German, Turkish, each with a separate model. Paraphrase/bypasser detection English-only. [S5][S9][S32]

**Indicator states and the 20% threshold.** States: 0%; asterisk (*%) for 1-19%; 20-100% shown with blue highlights; grey (--) could not process; error (!). "Our testing has found that there is a higher incidence of false positives when the percentage is between 0 and 19 ... To avoid potential incidence of false positives, no score or highlights are attributed for AI detection scores above 0% and below the 20% threshold ... If your report was generated before July 8, 2024, then you may still see a numerical score below 20%." [S9] History: May 2023, asterisk added but number still shown ("educator feedback was consistent: they did not like the idea of hidden AI percentage scores because it takes away their opportunity to have more information"); 16 July 2024, "Scores below 20% are no longer surfaced." [S4][S32] Admin statistics show these as "grey-striped bars represent reports with weak AI signals." [S46]

**Visibility.** Score visible only to instructors/admins; students cannot see it, though instructors can download the PDF and share it. Admins can disable the feature account-wide (not possible at launch: "For this first iteration, we're unable to suppress the AI writing detection indicator"). At launch it was on by default; Chechitelli said in Sept 2023 that 98% of customers used it and only 2% of institutions had disabled it by end of July 2023. [S3][S5][S11][S47]

**AI paraphrasing detection.** 6 Dec 2023 release note: "We now detect likely AI-generated text even if it may have been paraphrased using an AI word spinner ... There are no visual changes to the report." 16 July 2024: the report split into two interactive categories, "AI-generated only" (blue) and "AI-generated text that was AI-paraphrased" (purple), described as "qualifying text that was likely AI-generated and then was likely revised using an AI-paraphrasing tool or AI word spinner, such as Quillbot." Chechitelli: "Applying AI paraphrasing to AI-generated content is likely a sign that the writer is trying to hide their use of AI." [S32][S33]

**AI bypasser ("humanizer") detection.** 27 Aug 2025: "existing AI writing detection capabilities now include AI bypasser detection, designed to help educators detect text that may have been intentionally modified by AI humanizer tools." Folded into the "AI-generated only" category. English-only; Turnitin refuses to name the tools detected ("Sharing a list of tools that are detected would make it easier for students to evade our system"). Chechitelli to Plagiarism Today: humanizers "leave a statistical trace that can be learned and detected." The same forum post said GPT-5 output could be identified in "early internal tests" although the model was "not specifically trained on GPT-5." [S5][S34][S48][S49]

**4 Aug 2026 simplification.** The purple category was removed: "the signals that separate text that may be purely AI-generated from AI-generated text that may have been further modified by AI bypassers or paraphrasers have become more difficult to interpret." Stated goals include "Reduce the risk of unwarranted academic misconduct reviews by removing distinctions that could be read to imply more certainty about the specific tool or workflow used." Chechitelli's blog: "AI refinement tools have blurred the distinction between AI-generated and AI-modified text." [S35][S50]

**Model update cadence (vendor release notes).** 24 May 2023 (intro/conclusion aggregation, segment boundary fix); 9 Aug 2023; 6 and 15 Dec 2023 (a bug let <300-word docs be scored for 9 days); 16 July 2024; 12 Sept 2024 (Spanish, GPT-3.5/4); 2 Apr 2025 (Japanese: GPT-4 0613, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini); 27 Aug 2025 (bypasser); 14 Oct 2025 and 12 Feb 2026 ("improve recall while maintaining a low false positive rate"); 5 May 2026 (Spanish: GPT-5.x, Gemini 2.5); July 2026 (single model); 18 Aug 2026 (Arabic, trained on GPT-5.2-5.4, Claude Opus 4.5/4.6, Gemini 3.x, DeepSeek, Grok, Llama 4, o3, Mistral, Qwen); 6 Oct 2026 (Turkish, German, Dutch). Every update note says previously generated reports are not rescored unless resubmitted, and "this may result in higher AI scores on new submissions." [S32][S35]

**Turnitin Clarity (2025-2026).** Announced 4 Mar 2025 at SXSW EDU; GA as paid add-on Q3 2025. A composition space in which "educators can see their students' entire drafting process - such as pasted text, typing patterns, construction time and draft history," with video playback and an optional instructor-enabled AI assistant. From 12 Nov 2025, pasted blocks over 300 words get the AI writing score inside the Writing Report; timestamps added 12 Aug 2026. Turnitin frames this as "moving the focus from detection to transparency." AI writing scores also appear in the Authorship Report from 8 Jan 2026 (user must accept a disclaimer first). [S35][S36][S51][S52]

## Measured accuracy and independent evaluations

**Vendor claims timeline.**
- 13 Feb 2023 press release: "identifies 97 percent of ChatGPT and GPT3 authored writing, with a very low less than 1/100 false positive rate" (lab). [S1]
- 4 Apr 2023 press release: "Turnitin's technology has high accuracy and low false positive rates" (Chechitelli); PR Newswire version and the March 2023 FAQ carried the "98% confidence" / "<1% false positive" figures. [S2][S3]
- 23 May 2023 CPO blog: 38.5M submissions processed by 14 May; 9.6% over 20% AI, 3.5% over 80%. "Prior to our release, we tested our model in a controlled lab setting ... Since our release, we discovered real-world use is yielding different results from our lab." 800,000 pre-ChatGPT samples run. "For documents with over 20% of AI writing, our document false positive rate is less than 1% ... Our sentence-level false positive rate is ~4%." Of false-positive sentences, 54% sit "right next to actual AI writing," 26% two sentences away, 10% three away. "Our metrics may change, particularly those related to the 20% AI writing threshold, the 300 word count requirement, and ~4% sentence-level false positive rate." [S4]
- 26 Oct 2023 ELL blog: "<1% for documents with 20% or more AI writing"; "slightly higher-than-comfortable false positive rate on short submissions (fewer than 300 words)." [S10]
- 16 July 2024: "Of the over 200 million papers reviewed since the launch ... Over 22 million papers (approximately 11 percent) have at least 20 percent AI writing ... Over six million papers (approximately 3 percent) have at least 80 percent." [S33]
- 12 Sept 2024 blog: "over 250 million submissions have been reviewed, with 8.4 million flagged as having at least 80% potential AI writing." [S45]
- 2026 FAQ: "<1% for documents with over 20% of AI writing ... before every update or new model release, we perform tests on over 700,000 additional academic papers that were written before the release of ChatGPT." No published recall/true-positive figure. [S5]
- 24 Feb 2026 press release: "an average of 14.8% of English language submissions to its latest version of the AI detection tool had 80% or more AI-generated writing between October 2025 and February 2026," vs 3.3% Apr-Aug 2023. [S36]
- Turnitin to SF Standard (May 2026): acknowledges a +/-15 percentage point variance on scores (vendor statement relayed by journalist; the 2023 FAQ's "up to 15%" under-count is the likely origin). [S42]

**Independent tests.**
- *Washington Post, Fowler, 1 Apr 2023* (read via TRAILS republication and IHE/K-12 Dive): 16 samples (real student essays plus ChatGPT essays made with 5 high-school volunteers); "Turnitin identified over half of them at least partly incorrectly, including saying one student's completely human-written essay was written partly with AI" (Lucy Goetz, Concord High School). Wikipedia and a USD law-library guide characterise this as a "50%" false-positive rate on a small sample. [S8][S19][S53]
- *Weber-Wulff et al., Int. J. Educational Integrity 19:26, Dec 2023 (arXiv 30 June 2023)*: 14 tools, 54 documents in 6 classes (human, machine-translated, two AI classes, manually edited AI, machine-paraphrased AI). Turnitin ranked 1st: binary accuracy 41/54 = 76%; 0 false positives on the 18 human/translated texts ("The probability of false positives ranged from 0% (Turnitin) to 50% (GPT Zero)"); but false negatives on 10/36 AI documents (27.8%), concentrated in manually edited (4/9) and machine-paraphrased (6/9) classes. Conclusion: "all tools scored below 80% of accuracy and only 5 over 70%"; "As the detection tools for AI-generated text are not reliable, a prevention-focused approach" is recommended. Turnitin provided the researchers a demo login. [S14]
- *Temple University (Salem, Fiore, Kelly, Brock), undated institutional report c. 2023*: 120 samples (30 each human, AI, "disguised" via free spinners, hybrid). Human: 28/30 correct (one flagged at 11%, one unrated). Fully AI: 23/30 scored 100% AI (77%), 5 partial (52-97%), 2 unrated. Disguised: 19/30 (63%). Hybrid: 13/30 scored between 1-99% (43%); on a "substantially correct" reading, 23-57%. "When we evaluated the 'Flags' reports, we found no relationship at all between sections of the paper that were flagged as AI generated, and the parts that actually were generated by AI." 90 samples resubmitted twice gave consistent scores. [S16]
- *Perkins, Roe et al., arXiv 28 Mar 2024; IJETHE 21:53 (2024)*: 7 detectors incl. Turnitin, 805 tests, text from GPT-4, Claude 2, Bard, with 6 adversarial techniques. Baseline accuracy (mean of 3 scoring methods): Copyleaks 64.8%, Turnitin 61% (2nd). After adversarial techniques Turnitin fell from 50% to 7.9%, "the highest drop in accuracy (42.1%)," ranking 5th of 7. Error analysis: Turnitin 0 false accusations on 10 human controls (0%) but 88 undetected cases (84% UDC). Overall: "these tools cannot currently be recommended for determining whether violations of academic integrity have occurred." [S15]
- *Arvidsson, Discover Artificial Intelligence, 22 Dec 2025*: 797 intro physics lab reports (511 pre-ChatGPT as a false-positive baseline) run through Turnitin and GPTZero; 7.7% of post-ChatGPT reports contained generative-AI language. Both detectors "generally correlated with the presence of manually found generative AI language," but "Care must be taken to identify false positives, primarily due to the technical writing nature of physics lab reports and AI-based spelling and grammar tools." (Abstract read via OpenAlex; a widely repeated "2.3% Turnitin false-positive" figure could not be verified from the abstract.) [S17]
- *Van Vlasselaer, Van Droogenbroeck & Spruyt (VUB), J. Educational Integrity 22:16, 29 June 2026*: GPTZero, Pangram, Copyleaks, Turnitin on 160 synthetic papers (>4,000 words; 40 each human/ESL, fully AI, hybrid, humanised). Abstract: "the other tools significantly underestimated GenAI content, particularly for texts generated with the most advanced model. All tools correctly identified fully human texts ... False positives were rare across all tools." Per-tool detection rates as relayed by Pangram's blog (vendor-relayed, unverified against the paper): Turnitin 0% fully-AI, 60% hybrid, 52.5% humanised, 0% FPR; Copyleaks 0/32.5/25; GPTZero 0/0/2.5; Pangram 97.5/95/95. The abstract notes Turnitin's sub-20% asterisk band is where its fully-AI scores landed. [S18][S54]
- *Karr, Khvatskii, Hua, Chawla, arXiv 6 Aug 2026 (ACM AI Leadership Summit)*: "commercial AI detectors" (unnamed) on published abstracts: 9-15% of unmodified 2023-2025 human abstracts flagged (non-STEM far higher, p<0.001); light "refine abstract only" AI edits flagged 38-80%; humanized AI rewrites flagged under 4%. "Honest AI-editing results in a higher sanction risk than humanizer-assisted evasion." [S55]
- *Park, Jeong, Kim, EMNLP 2026 (arXiv 27 Aug 2026)*: 13 detectors, 135,389 pairs of non-native manuscripts and their professionally edited versions; FPRs on human text "ranged from 0.0% to 100.0%"; "professional editing style [is] a key confounding variable in AI detector outputs." Turnitin not named. [S13]
- *Jisc National Centre for AI, 24 June 2025*: review of 2024+ studies; "mainstream tools such as Turnitin and CopyLeaks have no trouble identifying text that is purely AI-generated and left unmodified ... However, these tools are relatively easy to circumvent"; figures "should not be used to determine the current effectiveness of any product." [S56]
- *HEPI, 20 July 2026*: three of four UK OIA (ombudsman) case summaries published July 2025 involved international or second-language students flagged by Turnitin; three were upheld or partly upheld against the university; in one, the student "was not shown the evidence before their viva." [S12]

**Vendor bias study (not independent).** Turnitin's 26 Oct 2023 evaluation (D. Adamson) sampled up to 2,000 texts per cell from public L1/L2 corpora (ASAP, PELIC, ICNALE), not in training data. "For documents meeting our minimum 300 word count requirement, the difference in the false positive rate (FPR) between L2 English writers ... and L1 English writers ... is small, and not statistically significant ... although the FPR for each group is slightly higher than our overall target of 0.01 (1%), neither group's FPR is significantly different from this target. Conversely, for documents that are 'too short,' ... it's significantly greater than our target." It critiques Liang et al. as "a small collection of works of just 91 TOEFL practice essays, all of which are less than 150 words long." Note the ELL samples were "short persuasive or informative writing tasks"; "A comparable collection of publicly available full-length university level documents from L2 and L1 writers was not available." [S10] Chechitelli to The Markup (Aug 2023): the tool "learns that more complex writing is more likely human." [S11]

## Documented failures and false positives

- **Launch-period false positives (2023).** WaPo's test flagged Lucy Goetz's fully human essay; Turnitin's May 2023 post conceded "real-world use is yielding different results from our lab," a "higher incidence of false positives" under 20%, and in "the first few or last few sentences of a document." UC Davis senior Louise Stivers had a court-case brief flagged by Turnitin (then in beta/"early access") and was sent to judicial affairs; cleared with Google Docs history (Rolling Stone, 2023). [S4][S8][S57]
- **Turnitin's own list of what trips the model.** "Sometimes false positives ... can include content without a lot of structural variation, text that literally repeats itself, or text that has been paraphrased without developing new ideas." Short texts give "all or nothing" predictions. Grammarly basic checks are said not to trigger it, but Arcadia's help desk (2026) warns "advanced 'AI-rewrite' features in Grammarly or Microsoft Editor may contribute to a higher percentage," and SF Standard cites a 2025 study where a Grammarly-edited human abstract scored 16%. [S5][S30][S42]
- **Dec 2023 bug.** For 6-15 Dec 2023, documents under 300 words were scored despite not meeting requirements; Turnitin warned such scores were "likely less accurate." [S32]
- **University of Waterloo (Sept 2025).** ITMS testing found "in more than one instance the product flagged human written text as 100% generated by AI, which is concerning"; the tool was discontinued because "the costs ... outweigh the benefits." [S26]
- **Australian Catholic University (ABC, 9 Oct 2025).** ~6,000 misconduct referrals across nine campuses in 2024, ~90% AI-related; DVC Tania Broadley said "around one-quarter of all referrals were dismissed following investigation." Internal documents showed ACU "regularly relied on the AI report alone" despite Turnitin's warning and knew of problems "for more than a year" before dropping the tool in March 2025. A final-year paramedic student's case rested on a single report flagging 84% of his essay; a nursing student waited six months to be cleared with "results withheld" on her transcript. [S41]
- **Newby v. Adelphi University (filed Oct 2025; ruling Feb 2026).** Turnitin marked a first-semester history essay as fully AI-written; two other detectors said human; the instructor called it "too advanced" for a first-year; the student (who has learning differences and worked with Bridge Program tutors) got a zero and a mandatory anti-plagiarism course. A judge ruled the plagiarism finding "without merit" and ordered it expunged; the family reported six-figure legal fees. (Sources disagree on whether the court was state or federal.) [S37][S38]
- **Kato v. Palo Alto USD (filed 5 May 2026, N.D. Cal.).** Turnitin reported 76% likely-AI on a sophomore's *Crucible* essay; the teacher relied on the score plus a disputed "Grammarly synonym" admission; in-class rewrite earned a D; the family produced Google Docs revision history and a 1,162-page packet; an assistant principal had a handwritten rewrite typed up and run through Turnitin without notice. The district has no published policy on what score triggers discipline. Suit alleges Title VI/IX discrimination and seeks to bar treating "AI scores as dispositive without educator review." [S42]
- **Leigh Burrell, University of Houston-Downtown (NYT, 17 May 2025).** Zero on an assignment worth 15% after a Turnitin flag, although Google Docs history showed two days of drafting; grade restored after a 15-page PDF of timestamped screenshots; she then filmed a 93-minute video of herself writing the next assignment. [S43]
- **Kelsey Auman, University at Buffalo (May 2025).** Three assignments flagged a month before graduation; 5 of 20 classmates got similar notices and two had graduations delayed; cleared after showing browser history; her petition to disable Turnitin's AI detection passed 1,000 signatures; UB said it "does not rely solely on AI-detection software." [S43][S44]
- **Yang v. University of Minnesota (2024-2026).** Expelled PhD student; evidence was faculty judgment plus ChatGPT comparisons and GPTZero (89%/19% on two answers), not Turnitin. Federal due-process claims dismissed Oct 2025; Minnesota Court of Appeals affirmed Feb 2026. Included for context: courts have so far deferred to institutional process when detectors are not the sole evidence. [S39]
- **Doe v. University of Michigan (filed 9 Feb 2026, E.D. Mich.).** Student with GAD/OCD accused three times based on "subjective judgments about Plaintiff's writing style" and "self-confirming 'AI comparison' outputs"; no detector named. [S40]
- **Common Sense Media survey (Sept 2024).** Self-reported false accusations: 20% of Black teens, 10% Latino, 7% white; ~79% of flagged teens said their work had also gone through detection software. A survey, not a detector-accuracy measurement; often misreported as a false-positive rate. [S58]
- **Arcadia University (2026).** After a Spring 2026 pilot of 13 workshops, Arcadia announced on 1 June 2026 it would renew ("A Starting Point, Not a Final Verdict"); by Oct 2026 its help page read "The University did not renew the AI detection due to updated terms in the TurnItIn AI contract." [S30][S31]
- **UK contract exits (THE, 9 Oct 2026).** Newcastle, Westminster and Southampton are leaving Turnitin; Westminster cited AI detectors that are "probabilistic rather than conclusive" with a "recognised risk of false positives"; Cambridge refuses EULA changes on data/IP. Turnitin: "Turnitin does not train a generative AI model, or LLM." [S59]
- **Institutional opt-outs and reasons (primary where available).** Vanderbilt (16 Aug 2023): 1% x 75,000 papers = ~750 wrongly flagged; "To date, Turnitin gives no detailed information as to how it determines if a piece of writing is AI-generated or not"; cites Stanford bias finding and OpenAI's shuttered classifier. UCLA (4 Apr 2023): "Due to concerns and unanswered questions around this new feature, we have temporarily opted out." Durham (June 2023): "We currently have no plans to use Turnitin's AI detection tool"; Cambridge likewise. Michigan State help page: "Turnitin AI Detection is not currently available at MSU"; MSU AI guidelines say detection tools are "generally discouraged"; Bloomberg (via The Register, Sept 2023) listed Vanderbilt, Michigan State, Northwestern and UT Austin as declining over false-accusation fears. UT Austin provost guidance: "The University does not endorse the use of AI Detection Software," holds no central contract, classifies it as High Risk Software that cannot be bought on a Procard, and warns that submitting student work to such tools without a contract "may violate FERPA" and student copyright. Inside Higher Ed (5 Aug 2026): "at least a dozen universities, including Northwestern, Georgetown, and New York Universities, have turned off Turnitin's AI detection software." Curtin (effective 1 Jan 2026): "This change is about fostering trust and clarity within a modern academic culture"; text-matching retained. GradPilot's tracker (July 2026) lists 22 institutions with primary statements for 10 and cites a community registry of ~66. [S20]-[S29][S60]

## Linguistic signals this research attributes to AI vs human prose

- Turnitin: AI text is "a little too probable, favoring repetition and unsurprising phrasing"; human writing "tends to be inconsistent and idiosyncratic, resulting in a low probability of picking the next word"; false positives cluster in "content without a lot of structural variation, text that literally repeats itself, or text that has been paraphrased without developing new ideas," in generic introductions/conclusions, and in short texts; "more complex writing is more likely human." [S3][S4][S5][S10][S11]
- Arvidsson (physics lab reports): six qualitative features of AI language: "verbose generality, fake concern, absence of numbers, suspicious formatting, significant 'waffling', and unrelated example"; technical/repetitive lab-report prose and grammar tools are false-positive sources. [S17]
- Weber-Wulff: machine translation of one's own text "can lead to a higher number of false positives"; manual editing and machine paraphrase of AI text defeat detectors. [S14]
- Park et al. 2026: professional editing toward native-like style changes detector scores in both directions; style, not origin, drives many flags. [S13]
- Karr et al. 2026: light AI "polish" of human text is flagged far more often than fully humanized AI text. [S55]
- Lawsuit complaints: formal tone, meticulous structure and consistency (Doe v. Michigan) and an essay being "too advanced" (Adelphi) were treated as AI evidence by instructors. [S37][S40]

## Open questions

- Turnitin has never published a document-level false-positive rate for the 0-19% band, a recall figure, or any audit of the 700,000-paper validation set; no third party has been given API access for standardized benchmarking (Bassett's request, 2025).
- Whether the July 2026 single-model consolidation and the Oct 2025/Feb 2026 "improve recall" updates changed false-positive behaviour on human text is untested outside Turnitin.
- The VUB per-tool figures (Turnitin 0% on fully AI papers) come via a competitor's blog; the paper's own tables were not accessible here.
- How much of the +/-15-point variance Turnitin now acknowledges to journalists is documented in product docs.
- Whether Arcadia's "updated terms in the TurnItIn AI contract" reflect a broader 2026 licensing change tied to the EULA dispute in the UK.
