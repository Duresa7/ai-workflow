I spent most of Tuesday trying to get a 2014 Thinkpad to boot off a USB stick, which is not what I had planned for Tuesday.

The plan was to write this issue. Instead I was on my knees in the hallway closet, because that's where the one working USB-A hub lives, swapping cables and muttering. My wife walked past twice. The second time she asked if the newsletter was "going well" and I said yes, which was a lie, but a small one.

Here's what happened. A reader (hi, Marcus) emailed last week asking whether I'd ever tried running the little note-taking tool I keep recommending on genuinely old hardware. Not old like "my 2021 MacBook feels slow." Old like a machine you'd find in a school district's e-waste pile. Fair question. I'd been telling people it was lightweight for two years and I had never actually checked.

So I checked. It took about nine hours, if you count the USB stuff, which I do.

Short version: it runs. It runs fine, actually. Startup is maybe four seconds on a spinning disk, and search over 3,000 notes comes back in under a second once the index warms up. The one thing that choked was the PDF preview, which I expected, and the one thing that surprised me was that sync didn't choke at all. I had assumed sync would be the problem. It wasn't. The problem was me not knowing that this particular Thinkpad needs legacy boot turned on in a BIOS menu that is three levels deep and labeled, I swear, "Startup."

I don't have a big lesson from this. Maybe a small one: I should test the claims I repeat. I've been saying "lightweight" the way people say "it's fine," as a reflex, and it turned out to be true, but I didn't know that until Tuesday night.

Next week I want to talk about the opposite problem, the apps that advertise themselves as minimal and then quietly eat 800 MB of RAM to display a to-do list. I have a list. It's not a short one.

If you've got an old machine in a closet somewhere and want to try this yourself, reply and tell me what you've got. I'll send the stick image. It's 1.9 GB and it works, eventually, even if your BIOS is labeled wrong.
